# HAQDAAR — Wayfinder Map

`wayfinder:map` · local-markdown tracker · charted 22 Aug 2026 · rev 18 (9 Sep)

## Destination

A HAQDAAR system design that is complete, trusted and sharp enough to be **cut into four owned modules and handed to other people to build** — target ~1 Sep 2026.

The map is done when nothing is left to decide before someone goes and builds.

## Notes

- **Domain:** inbound IVR for Indian government scheme discovery. Feature-phone caller, keypad primary, voice on top, 5–6 questions maximum, never says "you are eligible".
- **Acceptance bar (not a ticket — the standing test every decision is judged against):** on 12 Sep 2026, a live Indian number takes real calls in Hindi, English and Marathi; a caller who names a scheme gets it read back in under 20 s; a caller who describes themselves gets 2–4 named schemes; the LOG can prove why each was chosen. *(Tested against and held unchanged by T08, 1 Sep — see 'How a language gets added' below. **T12 (rev 15) pins the 20 s: it is measured to the first word of the scheme name, not to the end of the read-back.** **T16 (rev 16) pins "can prove why": the trace explains in text, it does not replay — audio is gone, so proof means a reader reconstructing the choice from one JSONL per call.** **T24 (rev 17) pins "in Hindi, English and Marathi": the caller speaks any mixture of the three on every turn, and the system speaks back in the one they chose.** **T14 (rev 18) pins the "under 20 s" to a measurement rather than an estimate: `t_name` runs from `vad.speech_end` of the opener utterance to the `playedStream` of a checkpoint placed immediately before the first word of the name — both ends are provider events, neither is a local guess.**)*
- **Build is out of the map.** This map produces decisions. Execution is tracked separately.
- **Every session:** grill-me + domain modelling. One ticket per session, except research tickets which run in parallel.
- **The existing 12 segments are evidence, not authority.** Every "lock" in them is provisional until it comes back through a ticket and Adarsh ratifies it. Mine them; don't inherit them.
- **Standing preference:** every question carries a default so nothing stalls. Verdicts, not option menus. Bullets, not paragraphs.
- **Ownership seam = the nine stages, grouped into four modules** — Audio (Telephony/Mouth/Ear), Model (Router+Reader), Engine (Checker/Filter/Planner), Data (Corpus+LOG). A person owns a module and its interface, not a document. Docs get re-cut to match.
- **Corpus source today is the scrape.** A government data feed is assumed to replace it after sale; nothing in the design may depend on that feed existing.
- **English myScheme content is the only source of truth** (rev 5). Hindi and Marathi text from myScheme is drafting material, never read-back material.
- **The truth lock is suspended for 12 Sep** (rev 11, T08): no human verifies translated strings before they are spoken. This is a recorded debt, not a repeal. Nothing built for the demo may assume it stays suspended.
- **The model contract is three fields** (rev 14, T11): `class`, `box`, `value` — no confidence number anywhere in the system, and no per-turn language. Every value is validated against the closed set **in code after the model returns**; the system prompt is not a guard. **Every value carries the transcript span that produced it, and code drops any value whose span is not literally in what the caller said.**
- **The box roster is seven** (rev 13, T10): box 0 `category` (free, from the opener) plus six askable — `state`, `gender`, `social_category` (**hard**), `age`, `income_band`, `occupation` (**soft**). **Hard means the caller cannot be wrong about it**, not that the law bars them. Hard boxes are walls; soft boxes are blur, and only blur is dropped when widening.
- **The LOG is one JSONL per call, one line per turn** (rev 16, T16): every turn writes a line whatever its class, and the trace **explains rather than replays** — audio is gone, so text reconstruction is the whole requirement. No mask, no survivor counts, no confidence, no summary object: everything derivable from the snapshot id and the box vector is derived on read. The exceptions written once at the moment they are true are the **ladder rung** and the **stop condition**. One file serves both the judge and the engineer.
- **The persisted transcript is personal data, and that is a debt** (rev 16, T16): the audio dies at hangup and the number is hashed, but a caste, an income band and a state spoken as a sentence survive in the trace, and are more identifying as text than the hash beside them. Kept anyway for 12 Sep, because a redacted transcript cannot evidence a CLARIFY or an UNCLEAR. Named on the same footing as T08's suspended truth lock — an accepted exposure, not an absent one.
- **There are two doors and one call** (rev 15, T12): Door A is not a stage in front of the router — it is the opener, with a **`scheme` pseudo-box** whose closed set is the corpus's scheme ids. The pseudo-box never enters the filter table, never gets a mask, is never asked and cannot be widened; it appears in the box vector for LOG reconstruction only. **Echo-confirm is unconditional for box values, and Door A is exempt** — the read-back is the confirmation and the keypad is the correction.
- **Code-mix in, chosen language out** (rev 17, T24): the caller may mix Hindi, English and Marathi freely on every turn — `mode="codemix"` is on for all three pins, always, and nothing depends on the flag because T08's English hop absorbs it anyway. The system's own speech is always the single language the caller picked at **turn 0** on the keypad, because Mouth serves pre-rendered files and no code-mixed file exists or ever will. **`*` re-pins at any moment and clears nothing; `#` is left free.**
- **The clock starts at `playedStream`, and the first input closes the turn** (rev 18, T14): Plivo's playback queue holds ~60 s, so the moment we finished *sending* a line is not the moment the caller finished *hearing* it — every line ships with a terminal `checkpoint` and every timer in the system hangs off its `playedStream`. **Our own budget is 1.2 s** from `vad.speech_end` to first byte on the wire, behind a **700 ms endpoint window**; the rest of a turn belongs to the caller. **The turn closes on the first complete input** — a keypress is complete on arrival, speech only at endpoint — which overturns "audio beats DTMF". **Speech barge-in is off**, replaced by *listen always, interrupt only on keypad*. **`#` repeats the last thing the system said**; like `*` it is a control key, costs no turn and no model call, and is bounded by the wall clock alone.

## Decisions so far

- **Destination named** (charting, 22 Aug): the design package, not the running system; 12 Sep is the acceptance bar.
- **Segments demoted to evidence** (charting, 22 Aug): nothing in 01–12 is final until re-ratified.
- **Ownership by module, not by segment** (charting, 22 Aug): segments are documentation slices and give two people the same code with no interface between them.
- **Mouth is TTS + an ingest-time cache** (charting, 22 Aug): one `say(text, language)` path; all repeated lines and all scheme read-back audio rendered once at ingest and served as files; only short echo fragments render live. Text always comes from code templates, never the model.
- **Barge-in policy** (rev 2, 22 Aug): keypad interrupts everything always; speech barge-in only if the provider streams live media (T01); an interrupt discards the remainder of the line; the final read-back is keypad-interruptible only. **T12 (rev 15) makes this load-bearing rather than convenient — it is the entire error-correction path for Door A.** **Resolved by T14 (rev 18): the conditional resolves to *available* and is declined anyway.** T01 delivered live media and Sarvam emits `vad.speech_start`, so the trigger exists — but capability was never the binding constraint, the trigger's reliability on 8 kHz household audio is, and the one long line was keypad-only already. Replaced by **listen always, interrupt only on keypad**: Sarvam hears the caller through the whole prompt (`audioTrack="inbound"`, our own audio is not on that leg), so the eager caller loses their answer nowhere — we simply decline to stop talking. Mechanism is `clearAudio` out, `clearedAudio` back, ~200–300 ms of overrun after the keypress.
- **LOG retention split** (rev 2, 22 Aug): audio destroyed at hangup; the decision trace persists because it *is* the proof the acceptance bar demands; caller number hashed or not stored. Overrides "everything deleted after the call", which would have made the success bar unprovable.
- **Dead-end ladder** (rev 2, 22 Aug; **widening clause superseded by T09, rev 12**): if nothing matches, say plainly that nothing matched and read the nearest two, labelled as nearest and not as matches. **The labelling is no longer a courtesy — T10 (rev 13) made it load-bearing and took ownership: the dropped box is named out loud, and the ladder rung is recorded in the LOG.** Model or ASR unavailable drops the call into keypad-only mode, which costs zero model calls. *"Widens the last mask and retries once" is replaced by T09's soft-only ladder — widening by recency can speak a scheme the caller is geographically barred from.*
- **"Anything else?" keeps caller facts** (rev 2, 22 Aug): a second question in the same call clears only the need box; state, age, occupation and other confirmed boxes survive.
- **Scale deferred, hosting not** (rev 2, 22 Aug): concurrency, autoscaling and cold-start behaviour are out of scope; a minimal deploy target for 12 Sep is a task ticket (T19).
- **Corpus snapshots are immutable and pinned per call** (rev 3, 22 Aug): the audio cache is content-addressed, so changed content simply produces a new file and nothing is ever invalidated. A call binds to one numbered corpus snapshot at connect and holds it to hangup; deploy builds a new snapshot, renders its missing audio, then flips a pointer. In-flight calls finish on the old snapshot. Orphaned audio is swept between deploys, never during a call.
- **Telephony is Plivo, India data region** ([[tickets/T01|Pick the telephony provider]], 23 Aug): decided by paperwork, not features — Plivo accepts a Udyam (MSME) certificate as business proof, obtainable in an afternoon with Aadhaar + PAN, where Exotel wants a Certificate of Incorporation we don't have. Twilio sells no Indian local DIDs at all; Ozonetel and Knowlarity are sales-led with nothing public to design against. Bidirectional WSS, μ-law 8 kHz, DTMF on the same socket. **The India data region binds to an organization and cannot be changed after that organization is created** — but a wrong pick is recovered by creating a new organization from the console switcher, not by re-signing up (corrected 28 Aug, T05 session; the original 'chosen at signup, cannot be changed' overstated it). No trial path exists — a paid, KYC'd account is required from day one. **Udyam alone satisfies KYC**; it is one of three accepted documents and no second document is required, though the first application must be sealed and signed. 022/080 review is now automated at ~5 minutes, not up to a business day.
- **[[tickets/T02|What myScheme actually gives us per scheme]]** (rev 5, 23 Aug): eligibility is prose and every filter value is ours to derive at ingest — the long pole is confirmed long. Only three fields arrive structured: level, application mode, tags. The four read-back chunks arrive pre-separated by section anchor, and **exclusions** arrives as a fifth separate block the Checker should use as negative rules. Segment 05's 18 boxes are confirmed as a real myScheme facet rail, not an invention — but the rail lives in the search wizard, never on the scheme record, so a scheme's position on any box is derived by us. Hindi and Marathi content exists and is disclaimed by the platform itself, so English is the source of truth and every spoken non-English line is human-verified by us. Third-party myScheme datasets must be spot-checked: at least one public one fabricates eligibility rules and Hindi text on fetch failure.
- **Sarvam is the ear, and the confidence ladder loses its number** ([[tickets/T03|Which ASR can hear Hindi, English and Marathi on a phone line]], rev 6, 23 Aug): Sarvam `saaras:v3-realtime` over an 8 kHz WebSocket, μ-law, language pinned per call. **T24 (rev 17) settles how that pin is set and defends it against auto-detect on grounds T03 did not have — the offline test base and the auditability of the trace, not accuracy alone — and turns `mode="codemix"` from an option into a standing requirement on all three pins.** Matches Plivo's leg exactly — no transcode between telephony and Ear. **No shippable ASR returns a per-utterance transcript confidence**, so the 0.50 / 0.85 gates have no source and are replaced by a match score our own code computes against the closed set for the slot — a number we own, tune, and can put in the LOG as proof. Deepgram Nova-3 named as fallback **provisionally: it is US-hosted and may not survive T20.**
- **Module owners write their own fakes first** (rev 3, 22 Aug): every owner's day-one commit is a fake implementation of their own interface returning deterministic values from one shared fixture file (one persona, three schemes). Consumers never write fakes for dependencies they don't own. All four modules are runnable against each other from day one, so interfaces fail on day one instead of day nine.
- **[[tickets/T04|What "working properly on 12 Sep" means]]** (rev 7, 25 Aug): ten calls, from outside the team, real handsets, ≥3 per language, judged after the fact from the LOG alone — eight of ten must pass. A call fails only on an untruth, an unprompted hangup, or ending before a terminal state; abandonment is read structurally off the LOG, never off tone. A correctly-executed dead-end (full widening ladder run, nearest-two honestly labelled) **is a pass** — this resolves the standing contradiction with "gets 2–4 named schemes." Outbound calling is ruled out of the map entirely, not a T04 option. **The 12 Sep scheme corpus is deliberately small, for testing ease** — not a shortfall against 50 or 100, which is a later-stage, post-bar decision against the full corpus. All spoken content, fixed lines and every in-corpus scheme, is tri-lingual at parity; no per-language subsetting. The LOG gains a second job: prove the scheme choice, and let a call's pass/fail be reconstructed without audio. **Discharged by T16 (rev 16), and it cuts the other way too: because T04 judges structurally off the LOG alone, no judge-only summary format is permitted — one file, two readers.**

- **Provisioning is parked behind the design work** ([[tickets/T05|Get an Indian inbound number in hand]], 28 Aug): T05 blocks nothing — its body claimed T14, but T14 is blocked by T24 alone — and the automated ~5 minute Plivo review removed the queue-time risk that put it on the frontier. It also proves nothing worth demonstrating: that an inbound IVR can take a call is not in question. **Latest safe start 5 Sep**, ratified 28 Aug: two to three days of work against seven days of runway, deliberately clear of the ~1 Sep design-package week so the two do not collide. The acceptance bar's live Indian number is held, not amended; if 5 Sep passes unstarted, amending the bar to allow the Twilio +1 becomes an explicit decision rather than a silent slip. Parking is safe only while the Audio module's provider seam stays honest — Twilio assumptions leaking past it turn a parked errand into a September rewrite.

- **The scheme record is facets, not rules** ([[tickets/T06|What one scheme record contains]], 29 Aug): one flat row per scheme, five field groups — identity/provenance, matching, filtration, negative rules, read-back. Each facet column holds a value from a closed list or `ANY`; the build expands every (column, value) pair into a scheme bitmask, so a turn is one AND instruction and there is no rule engine and no database in the call. Numerics are stored **exact** and become closed sets only at build — no banding, since the mask table is precomputed anyway. **Box 0 `category` is load-bearing**: a property of the scheme, read off the opening sentence, applied before question one, costing zero questions. **Aliases are a first-class field** in all three languages — a caller never speaks the official title. Exclusions subtract from a column's admitted set at capture where they can; where they cannot they become **`gate_notes`, a list, never spoken and never filtered on, carried into the LOG as declared blind spots.** One sentinel only, `ANY`; a second UNKNOWN was rejected because the build gate does that job better — a row that cannot be fully expressed **does not enter the snapshot at all**. `facets_verified` is tightened to mean a human read the eligibility prose and confirmed each `ANY` is real silence. **A sixth read-back chunk, `summary`, is added**: the bar wants a named scheme back in under 20 s, verbatim `benefit_text` is not 20 s in Marathi, and paraphrase of captured text is forbidden — so one authored ~35-word line per language, with the four verbatim sections offered by keypad after it. The record stores text only; audio stays derivative and content-addressed. No column may be deleted on the 16 Aug test numbers, which were measured against the dropped inferred corpus and are shape, not fact. **Amended by T12 (rev 15): the alias field gains a contract** — ≥3 per language, stored as spoken, with a build-time uniqueness gate and a build gate excluding schemes below the floor. **Amended again by T24 (rev 17): at least one alias per non-English language must be the code-mixed form**, because scheme names are proper nouns nobody translates and three pure-Marathi aliases are three strings no caller will ever say.

- **Three languages, no verifier, and the two legs stop sharing a rule** ([[tickets/T08|How verified content survives three languages]], 1 Sep): the ticket's 600-string arithmetic was stale on both terms — T06 made read-back five chunks (`summary` authored natively, not translated; aliases heard, never spoken; fixed lines a one-time block) and T04 already shrank N. **Design N = 100**, demo runs a small subset. **All three languages stay** — Marathi was briefly dropped for want of a verifier and restored the moment the verifier went, because that was the only thing making it expensive. **Inbound and outbound stop obeying the same rule**, because their failure costs differ: inbound goes caller's language → English internally → the model **selects one value from a fixed keyword list** → that keyword is echoed back in the caller's language from cache → confirm. The English hop is safe precisely because the exit is a closed set — the worst achievable error is the wrong item from a known list, recoverable by the ladder and visible in the LOG. **Outbound read-back stays pre-translated and cached, no runtime generation**, because an outbound error speaks a falsehood about money and is unrecoverable. **The keyword list is the test base** — ~150–200 values per language, frozen, making the inbound leg testable offline against a table of utterances; T22 now feeds Model as well as Data. **No human verifier is staffed**: translation ships naive, the truth lock is suspended as debt, and the system prompt forcing selection-from-database becomes the only remaining guard — which makes T11 load-bearing on the inbound path, not just read-back. The hold-aside check (names, amounts, dates byte-identical through translation) survives as a machine assertion, since it needs no language skill and catches the worst failure. Every staffing question in the ticket body is void with the verifier.

- **The masks are kept, not folded** ([[tickets/T09|The filter table and what a survivor mask means]], 1 Sep): `survivors = survivors AND mask` was the wrong shape — correct and insufficient. A set has no notion of *near*, so the dead-end ladder's "nearest two" and the bar's "2–4 named schemes" both had nothing to run on. A bare tally is also insufficient: a scheme missing **category** is worth reading with a label, a scheme missing **state** can never be had. So **turn masks are retained rather than ANDed away** — six answered boxes is six machine words, and from them derive **survivors** (AND, the truth claim), **tally** (how many masks hold the bit, ranks the near) and **miss-set** (*which* masks lack it, decides what may be spoken). Vector search is ruled out: it cannot produce a trace, and proving the choice is the LOG's second job. This amends T06's "one AND instruction" to **one pass, one word retained per turn.** A box holds a code, UNASKED or UNKNOWN — **UNASKED and UNKNOWN are the same instruction to the filter** (no mask appended) and differ only in the LOG and to T10; **UNKNOWN always widens, never narrows.** `category` is **box 0 in the same table**, not a pre-filter, because *"anything else?"* must be able to clear it. **There is no undo stack** — the box vector is the only state and everything derives, so a correction overwrites one box and re-derives; the ticket's recompute-vs-reverse question had a false premise. At zero survivors, **a scheme whose miss-set contains a hard box is never spoken**, and widening drops **soft boxes only**, one at a time in an order T10 supplies. **Amended by T11 (rev 14): UNKNOWN counts as *not satisfied* for T10's speaking rule, exactly as UNASKED does** — otherwise a caller who declines to state their state hears schemes they are geographically barred from. The only remaining difference is that UNASKED is still askable and UNKNOWN is removed from the askable pool forever. The Engine is **pure code, language-blind, with no runtime negation and no numerics** — Model selects a keyword, Data owns the list and the code map, and the code is the interface. **The snapshot id plus the ordered box vector reconstruct the entire call**, so the LOG stores no survivor sets. One code per box for 12 Sep. The single thing handed to T10 is the **hard/soft classification of every box.** *(T12, rev 15, removed the last place in the design that still wanted vector search.)*

- **Walls and blur, and a guarantee instead of an expectation** ([[tickets/T10|How the next question is chosen and when to stop]], 2 Sep): the ticket's default was overturned clause by clause. The **box roster had to be pinned first** — T09 asked for a hard/soft classification of boxes nothing had ever enumerated. Seven boxes: box 0 `category` free from the opener, then `state`/`gender`/`social_category` hard and `age`/`income_band`/`occupation` soft. **Hard means fixed and unmistakable to the caller, not legally barred** — the barred reading was tried first and made six of seven hard, leaving T09's soft-only ladder one rung and decorative; the *fixed* reading gives it four. **Information gain is rejected**: an expected-value score needs a caller prior we do not have, and a uniform prior degenerates into a proxy for how many values a box has, so `state` (36) would outrank `gender` (2) on every turn of every call. Replaced by **minimax elimination ÷ expected turns** — score a box by its worst answer, which puts a *guarantee* in the LOG ("cuts at least 47 whatever they say") rather than an expectation. **A question is not one turn**: keypad costs 1, spoken costs 2 because of the echo-confirm (T23), so the keypad preference is arithmetic and the tie-break bonus is unnecessary; ties break on snapshot order. **No question is mandatory** — `state`-always-first is replaced by a speaking rule of the same shape as T09's: *a scheme non-`ANY` on an unasked hard box may not be spoken*, which closes the UNASKED-widens hole without spending four turns up front. **Three stops**, not two: ≤4 survivors, **8 turns** (hard wall 6 questions), and **no remaining box splits the survivors** — the zero-information stop was missing from the ticket and is the one that stops pointless questions. **Stop at 1 and speak it**; do not widen upward to manufacture a second, because "2–4" is a delivery shape and T04 already judges structurally. **Tally cannot order survivors** — it is flat across them by construction — so T10 supplies **specificity**, the count of non-`ANY` boxes matched, without which the most generic scheme in the corpus tops every list on every call. Widening order: `income_band` → `age` → `occupation` → `category`, least-trusted first. At the cap with >4 survivors, top 3 by specificity spoken **as matches**, plus one line that more matched than could be read. The 8 is the only number here with no derivation behind it and is the dial to turn if the demo runs long. **Amended by T11 (rev 14): the ladder starts from whatever the opener filled and the caller confirmed, not from an empty box vector.** The scoring is unchanged — minimax ÷ expected turns already runs over remaining boxes and current survivors — it just needs a seeded start. Two favourable knock-ons: the speaking rule is already satisfied for any hard box the opener filled (typically `state`, the wall T10 refused to make mandatory), and the budget becomes opener 2 + 6 remaining, which is the shape T10 designed for.

- **Three fields, five classes, and the span is the guard** ([[tickets/T11|The closed set the model is allowed to say]], 2 Sep): the ticket was stale in three of six bullets — T09 had answered out-of-set, T10 had pinned the vocabulary, and T08 had changed the *shape* of the confidence question by making the model **select** rather than our code string-match, leaving T03's replacement number with no source either. **The model emits `class`, `box`, `value`.** `language` struck (T08 pins it per call; mid-call switching is T24's alone — **and T24 (rev 17) ruled `META` is not the route after all: hearing a request to switch requires understanding a caller you have just established you cannot understand, so the switch is a keypad affordance costing no model call**). `confidence` struck — a model scoring its own selection is a second generation, not evidence; replaced by **unconditional echo-confirm**, which T10 had already priced at 2 turns. **T23 inherits a ladder with no number and needs none.** **Five classes, not seven**: `ANSWER` / `CLARIFY` / `REPEAT` / `META` / `UNCLEAR`. CORRECTION is an ANSWER pointing at an earlier box (T09: no undo); NOISE never reaches the model, since an empty transcript is caught by code first. **CLARIFY and REPEAT are not merged** — *"kya matlab?"* needs different words, a bad line needs the same words, and on this corpus the confused caller is the common case. Precedence **META > ANSWER > CLARIFY > REPEAT > UNCLEAR**, as code rules on the output: losing a value is worse than delaying an explanation, and carrying on in a language the caller cannot follow is the one actively harmful continuation. **No class error can produce an untruth** — only a wrong *value* can, and the closed-set gate bounds that to the wrong item off a known list. **The opener is a different call shape**: it returns a *list* of (box, value, span), every question call returns exactly one pair. **The span guard is the load-bearing addition** — a model told to fill boxes freely will hear "farmer" and helpfully add "low income", which narrows, excludes schemes the caller qualifies for, and enters the LOG as though spoken. Code drops any value whose span is not literally in the transcript. **One confirm covers the whole opener bundle**, so it costs 2 turns whatever it fills and T10's arithmetic is unamended; on "no" every opener box drops to UNASKED and the ladder recovers them at normal price. **`UNKNOWN` is a reserved value in every box's set, not a sixth class** — it needs a span like anything else, it covers both "don't know" and "won't say", and it is **accepted first time and never pushed to keypad**, which matters most for caste. **Two consecutive non-ANSWER turns on a box drops that box to keypad** (new input to T18); a declination is not a failed turn. **Paid Gemini Flash-Lite class**: free tiers fail on concurrency — Groq's 6,000 TPM is one and a half phone calls, and Gemini free allows ~2 simultaneous callers against T04's ten outside handsets. The whole demo is ~40,000 tokens, so **billing costs cents and deletes the largest runtime failure mode**; free tier keeps its role as T08's offline test base. **Two model failures drop the call to keypad-only — do not limp**, because T04 scores terminal states and keypad-only is one, while a call that burns the cap retrying ends with nothing and fails. **No second model provider**: the fallback is the keypad, which cannot be wrong. **The model sees five things and a 2-turn window, never full history** — full history was proposed and the *cost* objection withdrawn (a Marathi call is ~600–1,200 tokens, cents, ~50–100 ms), but it **guts the span rule** by widening the haystack (a brother mentioned in Bihar at turn 1 passes the span check for `state` at turn 5) and **breaks T08's offline testability** by turning a table of utterances into a tree of sequences. The window gives corrections and pronouns; **the span check stays scoped to the current utterance only.** A per-box ask-counter fixes rephrase loops for ~10 tokens. **Amended by T12 (rev 15): echo-confirm is unconditional *for box values*. Door A is exempt** — a wrong box stamp is silent and compounds, a wrong scheme is loud and self-correcting, and confirming a name before saying the name catches no error it could not catch two seconds later.

- **Door A is the opener, and there is nothing left to fail** ([[tickets/T12|Door A: matching a spoken scheme name]], 4 Sep): the ticket was stale in three of its four bullets and misfiled as a prototype. **Embedding died at T09**; **the confidence threshold died at T11**; and only the *measurement* was ever blocked by T07 — everything else was unblocked design sitting behind a corpus it did not need. The reframe: **Door A is not a search stage in front of the router, it is the opener.** T11's opener already returns a *list* of `(box, value, span)`, so adding a **`scheme` pseudo-box** whose closed set is the corpus's scheme ids collapses the whole ticket into machinery that exists — no routing decision, no second model call, no extra turn. *"Fails cheaply into Door B"* stops being a claim needing a test and becomes *there is no failure path*: it is one call, and the doors are just which fields came back. The pseudo-box **never enters the filter table**, gets no mask, is never asked, cannot be widened, and appears in the box vector for LOG reconstruction only. **Two searches, not four** — exact alias string-match in code (zero cost, and T08's offline test base) and model selection from the alias set; **fuzzy was never a stage**, because the span guard compares the span against the *transcript*, not against an alias, so a mis-heard *"PM kissan"* passes and the model still selects the right id. **Echo-confirm is narrowed to box values and Door A is exempt** — the read-back is the confirmation, keypad barge-in is the correction, and a confirm turn costs 12–15 s of dead air against a 20 s bar to catch an error the first two seconds of the read-back catches anyway. **The 20 s clock is pinned by stamping both readings**: `t_name` (to the first word of the scheme name) **is the bar**, `t_end` (to the end of `summary`) is recorded but cannot fail it — so Marathi `summary` length becomes evidence for free instead of a surprise. Predicted `t_name` 3–5 s, since there is no TTS in the path. **T14 (rev 18) turns the prediction into a measurement**: the origin is `vad.speech_end` of the opener utterance and the stamp is the `playedStream` of a checkpoint placed immediately before the first word of the name — both ends are Plivo/Sarvam events, and the ~60 s playback queue means a locally-timed stamp would have measured our send, not the caller's ear. **Ambiguity is settled by candidate count** — 1 read, **2 → one keypad turn naming both**, 3+ → Door B seeded — and the cap of 2 falls out of the **alias uniqueness gate**, not out of code: an alias on ≥3 schemes is a category word and is dropped from all of them, an alias on exactly 2 *is* the disambiguation pair. So no ambiguity list is ever authored. Rung 2 at +6 s is the presence line, **the only audio T14 adds to T15: one fixed line × 3.** Rung 3 at +6 s plays the existing closing line and hangs up. **Turn 0 runs a 4 s gap, not 6**, because choosing a language carries no cognitive load — it is a hearing question, not a recall question — and it fills in the interval T24's *two plays then default Hindi* left unstated. **No silence timer runs during a read-back**; the caller is not expected to speak and the only clock is the ceiling. **"Audio beats DTMF" is overturned on arithmetic, not on taste**: DTMF arrives at keypress and a transcript arrives only after endpointing, so audio-wins requires holding every keypress for the endpoint window to see whether speech follows — which makes T10's *keypad costs 1 turn* false, since keypad would then pay the same wall clock as speech on every turn of every call. **The turn closes on the first complete input**, a keypress being complete on arrival and speech only at endpoint. On DTMF we send Sarvam `{"event":"flush"}` and write the finalised text as **`discarded_transcript`**, so the discard is evidenced rather than silent — T16's rule that the transcript is the only evidence a non-stamping turn leaves. **`*` and `#` are control keys, never answers and never in a menu**; digits 0–9 are answers and close the turn. **`#` repeats the last thing the system said**, the keypad twin of T11's `REPEAT` class: it cannot be misheard, costs no model call and no turn, and it hands the caller rung 1 of the silence ladder on demand — which is what makes *listen always, interrupt only on keypad* affordable. At turn 0 `#` re-plays the greeting **without consuming one of T24's two plays**, and is bounded by the wall clock, discharging T24's handoff exactly. **The ceiling is 10 minutes, not 5**: five cuts off a legitimate call, since turn 0 plus 8 turns plus a read-back with sections plus an *"anything else?"* round runs past six. Ten is a safety net for a wedged socket or a handset in a pocket — **nothing in the designed path reaches it, which is the point**, and a ceiling that fires on 12 Sep is a bug report rather than a caller. It reuses the existing closing line and adds no inventory. **`keepCallAlive="false"`**, so a dead socket ends the call instead of stranding a caller on a silent line — both outcomes fail T04, but only one of them is cruel; marked **ratify-on-hardware**, the single T14 ruling that must be confirmed on the first real call. **T16's turn-count agreement is discharged mechanically rather than by convention**: `vad.speech_start` fired with an empty or span-less final is **NOISE**, which consumes a cap turn and counts toward T11's two-non-ANSWER drop; no `speech_start` at all is **SILENCE**, which consumes neither and writes a line carrying the current `turn_n` and a `silence_n`. So the silent caller is bounded by the ladder and the noisy caller by T11's keypad drop, and those are the right two bounds for the two different failures.

## How a language gets added

Written plainly, because this is the thing that decides whether three languages is ambitious or routine. It is routine.

**Nothing in the system is written per language.** There is no Hindi code path and no Marathi code path. There is one path, and the language is a setting the call carries.

A language is two lists and a batch job:

1. **The keyword list** — about 150–200 words: the states, the occupations, the categories, the caste and income groups. Written once in that language. **Plus the scheme aliases** (rev 15, T12) — three per scheme, written the way callers actually say them.
2. **The scheme text** — every scheme's four sections plus the fixed lines the system says on every call, machine-translated and turned into audio files ahead of time.

Both are done before anyone calls. Neither touches the code. That is why **adding a language is roughly a day**, and why dropping one buys almost nothing.

**Inside a call it works like this:**

- The caller's language is set by one keypad press before anything else happens — one file plays three short clauses, one per language, and `*` changes it later at any moment
- **`#` says it again.** At any point, in any language, the caller can press one key and hear the last thing we said repeated. It costs nothing, it asks nothing of the model, and it is the reason we never cut ourselves off when the caller starts talking — if they missed it, they can ask again
- **They may mix all three languages freely, every turn** — nothing forces them into a lane, and what handles the mixing is the English hop below, not a setting
- They speak, and Sarvam writes down what they said
- We turn it into English inside the system, where all the thinking happens
- The system is only allowed to answer by **picking a word off our list** — it may not write its own
- That picked word is played back **in the one language they picked**, from a file we made earlier, so they hear exactly what got ticked and can correct it — the system never mixes languages when it speaks, because there is no mixed file and never will be
- When we finally read a scheme out, the audio was made and stored days ago — nothing is written or translated while the caller is on the line

**The one rule that holds all of this up:** the system chooses from a list going in, and reads from a file going out. That asymmetry is also why the caller may mix and the system may not. It never makes up a sentence. That is what stops a wrong translation from becoming a false promise about money, and it is also what lets us test the whole thing offline without making a phone call.

**What we are knowingly skipping for 12 Sep:** nobody reads the translations before they go out. On a real system a person would. For the hackathon the machine's word is taken as-is. This is written down as a debt, not forgotten.
