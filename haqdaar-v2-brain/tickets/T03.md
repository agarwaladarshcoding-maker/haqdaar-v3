# T03 · Which ASR can hear Hindi, English and Marathi on a phone line

**Type:** `wayfinder:research` (AFK) · **Module:** Audio · **Blocked by:** — · **Blocks:** T11, T14
**Status:** closed 23 Aug 2026 · **Asset:** [ASR candidate findings](../research/T03-asr-candidates.md)

## Question

Which ASR can transcribe Hindi, English and Marathi from **8 kHz narrowband telephony audio**, and does it return a usable per-utterance **confidence**?

Must establish, per candidate (Sarvam, Bhashini, Google STT, Deepgram, Whisper-hosted):

- Marathi support specifically — it is the weakest link of the three.
- Whether a confidence score is returned, and on what scale. The design's 0.50 / 0.85 gates are meaningless without one.
- Streaming vs batch, and end-of-speech detection latency.
- Behaviour on code-mixed speech, since a caller will mix Hindi and English inside one sentence.
- Cost per minute and free-tier limits.

## Why it blocks

The whole confirmation ladder — re-ask below 0.50, explicit confirm to 0.85, implicit above — is built on a number the ASR may not give us. If it doesn't, the ladder must be redesigned around something else.

## Default if this stalls

Sarvam for all three languages; if no confidence is returned, treat every speech turn as explicit-confirm and eat the extra turn.

---

## Resolution

**Sarvam `saaras:v3-realtime`, 8 kHz WebSocket, language pinned per call. It returns no transcript confidence, and the confirmation ladder is rebuilt on a match score we compute ourselves rather than one the ASR hands us.**

The default's first half survives. Its second half does not: explicit-confirming every speech turn was the wrong fallback, and this ticket found a better one.

### The finding that mattered

No candidate returns a usable per-utterance transcript confidence.

- **Sarvam** returns `language_confidence` only, and only when language is left on auto. That scores *which language was spoken*, not *how sure it is of the words*. Independently corroborated: the LiveKit Sarvam plugin hardcodes confidence to 1.0 because there is nothing else to read.
- **Google Chirp 3** returns a confidence field its own reference disclaims — not guaranteed accurate, not guaranteed present, `0.0` as the unset sentinel. Independent profiling calls it not confidence in the conventional sense.
- **Deepgram** is the exception and genuinely has the number, calibrated and word-level. It cannot be primary for other reasons (below).
- **Bhashini** does not document one. **Whisper** has only uncalibrated logprobs.

So the 0.50 / 0.85 gates had no source, in any candidate we would actually ship on.

### Why Sarvam

- Marathi is first-class, not an afterthought — the weakest link of the three languages is the one Sarvam markets as its differentiator.
- 8 kHz is native rather than tolerated: trained on compressed call-centre audio, and the realtime socket accepts `sample_rate=8000` with `mulaw`/`alaw` encoding, so no transcode sits between the telephony leg and the ear.
- Code-mixing is a named feature with its own output mode, not a hope.
- ₹30/hour billed per second, ₹100 free credits — the cheapest of the serious options and the only one with no rounding penalty on the very short turns this design produces.

### Why not the others

- **Deepgram** has the confidence number but is **US-hosted, which T20 may rule out**, and excludes Marathi from its real-time code-switching model. Hindi↔English mid-sentence works; Marathi↔English does not. Choosing it would trade a primary language requirement for a secondary mechanism. **Retained as the named fallback only provisionally** — if [[tickets/T20|T20]] rules that call audio may not leave India, the fallback is dead and must be re-picked (Google in `asia-south1`, eating the 15 s billing rounding, is the likely replacement).
- **Google** bills in 15-second increments rounded up, the worst per-call economics of the five for 5–6 short turns, and its Chirp 3 streaming language list is narrower than its batch list — Marathi would have to be confirmed there specifically, not from the marketing page.
- **Bhashini** degrades on exactly our two conditions: 8 kHz telephony and rapid Hindi–English switching. Keep as a cost and policy story for after sale, not as the ear.
- **Whisper** is ruled out on evidence: 73.8% WER on Marathi, no streaming, and a hallucination-on-silence failure mode that is actively dangerous in an IVR where silence is routine.

### What replaces the confidence gates

HAQDAAR never needs an open-vocabulary transcript. Every speech turn resolves to a value in a closed set — a scheme name, a state, an occupation, a yes/no. So **score the match, not the audio**: run the transcript against the allowed set for that slot, and gate on the top candidate's score and its margin over the runner-up.

This is strictly better than an ASR float, for three reasons:

- It is a number we own, tune, and can explain.
- It goes in the LOG as part of the proof the acceptance bar demands. A vendor's opaque confidence never could.
- It degrades into a *specific* re-ask — "did you say Ladki Bahin or Ladka Bhau?" — instead of a generic "sorry, say that again". Better behaviour on the caller's side, not just ours.

The thresholds themselves are not set here; that is [[tickets/T23|T23]].

### Configuration handed to the Audio owner

- `saaras:v3-realtime` over WebSocket, `sample_rate=8000`, `encoding=mulaw` — matches Plivo's bidirectional WSS leg exactly (T01), so no transcode sits between Telephony and Ear.
- `stream_type="fast"`, `endpointing="vad"`. Default `silence_duration_ms` is 500 ms and is likely too slow here — tuned in T14.
- `language_code` pinned per call to `hi-IN` / `en-IN` / `mr-IN`, not `auto`. Pinning costs the `language_confidence` field, which is no loss since nothing now depends on it, and buys accuracy. How the pin is chosen is [[tickets/T24|T24]].
- Barge-in drives off `vad.speech_start` or early partials, never off the final transcript.
- Mid-call `config.update` changes mode or language at an utterance boundary without reconnecting — relevant to T21.

### Caveat carried forward

Every accuracy claim here is a vendor's or a third party's, measured on their audio. Marathi on genuinely degraded rural 8 kHz is unverified. The cheap check — twenty recorded Marathi utterances through Sarvam and Deepgram at 8 kHz — should happen before T17 freezes the Audio interface. Flagged there, not ticketed separately.
