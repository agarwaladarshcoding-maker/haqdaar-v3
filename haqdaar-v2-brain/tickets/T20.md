# T20 · Whether call audio may leave India

**Type:** `wayfinder:research` (AFK) · **Module:** Audio · **Blocked by:** T03 · **Blocks:** T19

## Question

Plivo enforces **media anchoring** on domestic Indian calls: the media must reside in India,
and a domestic leg bridged to an international leg fails with `Violates Media Anchoring`.

Does streaming call audio from our own WebSocket server to a **non-Indian ASR or TTS
endpoint** breach that rule, or does anchoring bind only the telephony leg?

Must establish:

- Whether media anchoring is a carrier-level constraint on the call leg only, or a data-residency
  obligation that follows the audio wherever we forward it.
- Whether the chosen ASR (T03) offers an India region, and at what latency and price delta.
- Whether the same applies to Mouth's TTS, or whether ingest-time rendering sidesteps it
  entirely — audio rendered *before* any call exists is not call media.
- Whether Exotel's IP-PSTN region rule (Mumbai/Veeno instance) implies the same constraint,
  in case we fall back.

## Why it blocks

If anchoring binds the audio and not just the leg, the ASR shortlist collapses to
India-region providers and the service must run in an Indian region — which decides T19
rather than being decided by it.

## Default if this stalls

Assume it binds. Choose an India-region ASR and an India-region deploy target. The cost of
being wrong this way is latency and price; the cost of being wrong the other way is a dead
number on 12 Sep.
