# T04 · What "working properly on 12 Sep" means

**Type:** `wayfinder:grilling` (HITL) · **Module:** cross · **Blocked by:** — · **Blocks:** nothing formally, informs everything
**Status:** resolved 25 Aug 2026 — ratified by Adarsh, branch 5 revised on his correction

## Question

What exactly must be true on 12 Sep for this to count as working, and what may fail without it counting as failure?

To resolve:

- Who calls the number on the day — judges, a teammate, a real citizen, a stranger?
- How many calls, and does one bad call fail it?
- Which caller behaviours must be survived: silence, a wrong keypad press, a scheme that doesn't exist, someone who mixes languages mid-sentence, someone who says nothing intelligible.
- Is a caller who gets **no** match a pass or a fail?
- Marathi and Hindi at parity with English, or English carrying the demo?

## Why it matters first

"Properly" is currently undefined, and an undefined bar quietly expands to include everything. This ticket is what lets later tickets say no.

---

## Resolution

### The bar, in one paragraph

On 12 Sep 2026, ten calls are placed to a live Indian inbound number by people outside the team, from real handsets, at least three in each of Hindi, English and Marathi. Each call is judged afterwards from the LOG alone, pass or fail. **Eight of ten passes.** A call fails if and only if the system said something untrue, hung up unprompted, or ended before reaching a terminal state. Nothing else fails a call.

### Branch 1 — who calls · **closed**

- **Ten calls, callers from outside the team, real handsets, unrehearsed.** Not judges in the room; not a wild public line.
- **Dialability is a knob, not part of the bar.** Whether the number is publicly reachable on the day is a T05/T19 setting and changes nothing here. Default: live during the demo window only.
- **Outbound is ruled out of this map.** Outbound is a different product — the caller has no need in mind, Door A cannot exist, the greeting and consent line change, the 20 s clock starts from a caller who is not ready, and Indian outbound to unregistered numbers is a DLT/DND compliance surface this design has never accounted for. If outbound becomes real it is a redrawn destination, not a T04 option.

### Branch 2 — what fails a single call · **closed**

Judged **after the fact, from the LOG**, never live in the room. A call fails on exactly three conditions:

1. **The system said something untrue** — named a scheme the caller does not plausibly qualify for as a match, stated a benefit or document the corpus does not support, or asserted eligibility in any form.
2. **The system hung up unprompted.**
3. **The call ended before a terminal state.**

**Terminal state** is one of: read-back started · honest dead-end delivered · keypad-only fallback delivered. Hangup anywhere else is abandonment, and abandonment is a fail.

**Frustration is not the test — structure is.** "The caller shouted and cancelled" cannot be adjudicated, because audio is destroyed at hangup (rev 2) and the LOG is the only surviving artifact. Abandonment is therefore derived structurally: hangup event, stage at hangup, re-ask count on the live slot. This also hands the Engine a live signal — three re-asks on one slot is a caller about to leave, which is [[tickets/T10|How the next question is chosen and when to stop]]'s business.

**Not failures at this branch:** call length on Door B (measured and logged, no ceiling enforced on the day — [[tickets/T14|The turn clock]] has not run and inventing a number now creates a failure mode that cannot be tuned before September). Door A's 20 s read-back stands as a promise and is logged per call, but a single breach does not fail the demo — three do, via the eight-of-ten rule.

**Consequence for another ticket:** there is no "call analysis system". Reading the LOG is the analysis. But the LOG now has **two** jobs, not one — prove why each scheme was chosen, *and* let a reviewer reconstruct pass/fail for a call without audio. The second job goes to [[tickets/T16|What the LOG must prove]].

### Branch 3 — is a no-match caller a pass · **closed, ratified**

**A dead-end is a pass if and only if the widening ladder ran first.**

- Zero survivors → mask widened once → still zero → says plainly that nothing matched and reads the nearest two, labelled as nearest and **not** as matches: **pass**.
- Zero survivors → dead-end delivered without the widening step: **fail**. The mechanism is what is being judged, not the caller's luck.
- Reading the nearest two **as matches** is condition 1 of branch 2 — untrue — and fails.

This resolves the standing contradiction. The acceptance bar's *"a caller who describes themselves gets 2–4 named schemes"* is hereby read as **"gets 2–4 named schemes, or an honest correctly-labelled dead-end reached through the full ladder."** With 50 schemes and unbriefed callers, dead-ends are the common case, not the edge — plausibly two or three of the ten. The bar has to survive that or it is not a bar, it is a wish.

**Accepted consequence:** on 12 Sep a caller can correctly walk away with nothing, and that call counts as a pass.

**Edge ruled while here:** exactly **one** survivor is a pass. One named scheme plus an honest statement that it is the only one beats padding to two with something that does not match — padding is a lie and fails under branch 2.

### Branch 4 — behaviours that must be survived · **closed, ratified**

**Survived** means: the system reaches a terminal state, or issues a *specific* disambiguating re-ask, and never says something untrue and never hangs up unprompted.

Must be survived — and at least one call in the ten deliberately exercises each:

| Behaviour | Required response |
|---|---|
| **Silence** — no keypad, no speech | Re-prompt once with the keypad option stated explicitly; second silence drops to keypad-only; third ends the call politely as a delivered terminal state |
| **Wrong / out-of-range keypad press** | Named re-ask stating the valid range; never silently ignored |
| **Keypad mash** — several digits fast | First valid digit in range wins, remainder discarded; never an error |
| **A scheme that does not exist, or is outside the corpus** | Says plainly it does not have that one, then offers Door B. Never invents, never substitutes a similar name silently |
| **Mixed languages mid-sentence** | Handled under the pinned language; a failed parse becomes a specific re-ask, not a generic error. Depends on [[tickets/T24|How the call's language is chosen]] |
| **Nothing intelligible** | Match score below threshold → specific re-ask naming the closed set for that slot → second failure drops that slot to keypad |
| **Answering a different question than the one asked** | Match against the live slot's closed set only; a miss is a re-ask, never a wrong-slot write |

**Explicitly not required to be survived, and not counted against the demo:** carrier-side call drops, a caller who hangs up after a terminal state, a caller who hangs up in the first two seconds without speaking, DTMF a handset never transmitted.

### Branch 5 — language coverage · **closed, ratified with correction**

**Parity, and the corpus is deliberately small on 12 Sep — by choice, not by shortfall.**

- All fixed spoken lines — greeting, every question, every re-ask, the ladder, the dead-end wording, the read-back scaffolding — are human-verified in Hindi, English and Marathi. No exceptions. These are few, reused, and cached at ingest, so the bill is small and it is the bill that buys parity.
- **A scheme is in the 12 Sep corpus only when all four read-back chunks are human-verified in all three languages.** Verification is the entry condition, not a follow-up task.
- Therefore **one corpus, identical in all three languages.** No per-language corpus variation, no scheme reachable in English but not Marathi. That variant was considered and rejected: it makes a Hindi caller who names a real scheme hit a dead-end for a scheme that exists, which is honest but indefensible, and it doubles the state space every downstream module has to reason about.
- **English remains the source of truth** (rev 5). Hindi and Marathi are derived from English, never from myScheme's own disclaimed translations.

**Corrected framing for [[tickets/T07|Choose and structure the 50 schemes]]:** 12 Sep is a **testing-phase bar**, and the corpus is kept deliberately small on purpose, for ease of testing and clean tri-lingual verification — not because verification is a bottleneck to be pushed against. The full scheme corpus is a **later-stage** concern, arriving once the system is past this acceptance bar, in the same window the map already reserves for the government data feed replacing the scrape. T07 should size the 12 Sep set for what ten testers plus a caller-profile can exercise cleanly, not for coverage breadth.

**Consequence for the map's open "50 or 100" question:** it is not a 12 Sep question at all. Whatever the eventual production count turns out to be, it is decided later, against the full corpus, outside this bar. It should stay in *Not yet specified*, but reframed: not "which number do we pick," but "deferred past 12 Sep, testing corpus is intentionally smaller than either."

**This also sharpens branch 3.** A small deliberate testing corpus makes a caller landing on zero survivors *more* common on the day, not less — which is exactly why branch 3 ruled a correctly-executed dead-end a pass rather than a fail. The two decisions lean on each other: keep the corpus small for ease, and the dead-end path has to be a legitimate, provable outcome, not an embarrassment to be designed away.

### Branch 6 — the explicit non-failure list · **closed, ratified**

None of the following fails a call. This list exists so later tickets can say no to work that is chasing something the bar never asked for:

- ASR mishears and the system recovers through a specific re-ask
- Two of five slots needed a re-ask
- A Door B call ran long
- Keypad-only fallback was used end to end, model or ASR unavailable, and a terminal state was reached
- An honest dead-end after the full ladder
- Exactly one scheme named, or two rather than four
- TTS pronunciation imperfect but intelligible
- The scheme the caller wanted is simply not in the corpus
- Caller hung up after a terminal state
- The LOG shows a low match score, provided the system acted on it correctly

## What this ticket now lets later tickets refuse

- No live scoring, no judge-facing dashboard, no "call analysis system" — the LOG is the artifact. → T16
- No Door B time ceiling before T14 has a defensible number.
- No per-language corpus subsetting. → T07, T08
- No outbound path, no public-line hardening, no concurrency work for the demo. → out of scope
- No sizing the 12 Sep corpus against the eventual production count — it's a deliberately small testing set, not a scaled-down version of 50 or 100. → T07, T09

## Open, pushed downstream

- Exact re-ask wording and the match-score threshold per slot → [[tickets/T23|What the confirmation ladder runs on]]
- Size and selection of the small 12 Sep testing corpus, chosen against a caller profile rather than for breadth → T07
- Whether the language pin can change mid-call, which decides how "mixed languages" is actually survived → T24
- The eventual production scheme count (50 vs 100 vs other) — explicitly out of the 12 Sep bar, resolved later against the full corpus
