# T10 · How the next question is chosen and when to stop

**Type:** `wayfinder:grilling` (HITL) · **Module:** Engine · **Blocked by:** ~~T09~~ · **Blocks:** T13
**Status: CLOSED 2 Sep 2026**

## Question

What does "ask the highest-scoring question" mean arithmetically, and when does the loop stop?

To resolve:

- The score. Information gain over survivors, or something cheaper we can defend?
- Tie-breaking, and whether cheap-to-answer questions (keypad-able) get a bonus.
- The stop condition: ≤4 survivors **or** 6 questions spent — ratify both numbers, or change them.
- Are some questions mandatory regardless of score (state, almost certainly)?
- What is read back when 6 questions are spent and 20 schemes still survive?

**Three the body missed**, added at session open — all three are what T09 is actually waiting on:

- The **hard/soft classification of every box**, handed over explicitly by T09.
- **Survivor ordering** when more than four survive.
- The **order soft boxes are dropped in** during widening — T09 says "an order T10 supplies."

## Why it blocks

This is the difference between a call that ends in 90 seconds and one that ends in five minutes.

## Default if this stalls

Greedy information gain, keypad-answerable questions preferred on ties, state always asked first, cap at 6, read the top 3 by survivor score when the cap is hit.

*(Every clause of this default was overturned. Kept for the record.)*

---

## Resolution

### D0 · The roster had to be pinned first

You cannot classify "every box" hard or soft without a ratified box roster, and there was none. T09 said "six answered boxes is six machine words," T06 fixed box 0 as `category`, T02 confirmed an 18-facet rail on myScheme's wizard. Nothing named the six. T10 pins them.

### D1 · The box table

The axis is **fixed, not legally barred.** The first cut proposed *barred vs mismatched*, which made six of seven boxes hard and left T09's soft-only widening ladder with exactly one rung — decorative. Adarsh re-cut it: **hard means the caller cannot be wrong about it.** A thing they know, hold a certificate for, or could not misreport. Blurry, self-reported or borderline facts are soft.

| # | Box | Filled by | Class | Why |
|---|-----|-----------|-------|-----|
| 0 | `category` (the need) | opener | soft | a topic, not a fact |
| 1 | `state` | question | **hard** | fixed; caller cannot be wrong |
| 2 | `gender` | question | **hard** | fixed |
| 3 | `social_category` | question | **hard** | fixed, documented, caller knows it |
| 4 | `age` | question | soft | near-misses are real — 59 against a 60 pension |
| 5 | `income_band` | question | soft | self-reported, fuzzy, changes |
| 6 | `occupation` | question | soft | callers do more than one thing; T09 deferred multi-value |

Six askable, box 0 free. The ladder gets four rungs instead of one — that was the point of the re-cut.

### D2 · The score: minimax elimination ÷ expected turns

Score each unasked box by its **worst** answer — the value that would leave the most survivors. That count is the score; lowest wins. Then divide by expected turns.

**Information gain was rejected**, not on cost. Cost is a non-issue: ~100 schemes is two machine words and every candidate answer is one AND. It was rejected because an expected-value score needs a prior over what the caller will say, and we have none — no call history, no census weighting, cross-call memory is out of scope. A uniform prior over each closed set is the only available assumption and it is wrong: it turns entropy into a proxy for *how many values a box has*, so `state` (36 values) outranks `gender` (2) on every turn of every call regardless of corpus shape. Fake precision, and indefensible in the LOG.

Minimax puts a **guarantee** in the LOG instead of an expectation: *this question was chosen because it cuts at least 47 schemes whatever they say.* Slightly greedier than optimal, entirely reconstructible.

**A question is not one turn.** Keypad-answerable box = 1 turn. Spoken box = 2, because it needs an echo-confirm (T23 owns that ladder). Keypad-able: `gender`, `social_category`, `income_band`, `category`. Not keypad-able: `state` (36), `occupation` (~20). So the cheap boxes and the fixed boxes are almost disjoint sets, and `state` is both mandatory-ish and the most expensive thing on the board. Dividing by turns makes the keypad preference **arithmetic**, replacing the tie-break bonus the body asked about — nothing to justify separately.

Ties break on **snapshot order**. Deterministic, so the LOG reproduces the call exactly.

### D3 · No mandatory questions — a speaking rule instead

The body assumed `state` is always asked first. Overturned.

Hard boxes are asked first, in minimax order, **only while they still split the survivor set.** Asking gender when every survivor is `ANY` on gender is pure annoyance and buys nothing.

The truth guarantee moves to the read-back: **a scheme non-`ANY` on an unasked hard box may not be spoken.** Identical in shape to T09's ratified never-speak rule, so no new machinery. This closes the hole T09 opened — UNASKED and UNKNOWN are the same instruction to the filter and both widen, so an unasked `state` would otherwise let a state-barred scheme survive and be spoken, which is the one failure T04 calls fatal — **without** spending four turns of the budget up front to do it.

### D4 · Stop condition: three stops, 8 turns

Whichever fires first:

1. **≤4 survivors** — ratified unchanged.
2. **8 turns spent**, with a hard wall at **6 questions** regardless of turn count, honouring the map's standing 5–6 question note. Roughly 65 s of questioning at ~8 s/turn.
3. **No remaining box splits the survivors** — zero-information stop.

Stop 3 was absent from the ticket and is the one that actually protects callers from stupid questions. Without it the loop keeps asking until a counter runs out even when every remaining box is `ANY` across all survivors.

**Stop at 1 survivor and speak it.** Do not widen upward to manufacture a second. The bar's "2–4 named schemes" is a delivery shape, not a floor T04 fails you on — T04 already judges structurally.

The 8 is the one number in this ticket with no derivation behind it. Flagged as the dial to turn if the demo feels long; held at 8.

### D5 · Survivor ordering: specificity

**Tally cannot rank survivors.** Every survivor holds every answered box by construction, so their tallies are identical — T09 handed T10 a signal that only works at zero survivors. T10 supplies the substitute.

**Specificity = the count of non-`ANY` boxes the scheme matched on.** A scheme that survives because it is `ANY` on everything ranks last; the scheme actually targeted at this caller ranks first. Without this, the top of every list is the most generic scheme in the corpus, on every call.

Derived from masks already held. Stored nowhere. Tie-break: fewer `ANY` columns overall, then snapshot order.

### D6 · Widening order (soft boxes only, one at a time)

`income_band` → `age` → `occupation` → `category`

Least-trusted first, the caller's stated need last. Stop the moment ≥2 schemes are speakable. Hard boxes are never dropped — they are walls, not blur.

### D7 · Cap-hit read-back

- ≤4 survivors: read all.
- \>4 survivors at cap: **top 3 by specificity, spoken as matches** — they are true matches, every answered box holds — then one line stating more matched than could be read.

Both filtered through D3's unasked-hard-box rule before anything is spoken.

### D8 · "Nearest" labelling becomes a T10 rule

Making `income_band` and `age` soft means widening can surface a scheme whose statutory ceiling the caller exceeds. This is not an untruth **only because** of the labelling — so the labelling stops being a rev-2 courtesy and becomes load-bearing, owned here:

- Any scheme surfaced after a soft box was dropped is spoken as **"nearest," never as "matched."**
- The dropped box is **named out loud** — *"this one has an income limit you may be over."*
- The Engine marks it in the box vector, so the LOG shows **which rung of the ladder produced each name.**

## Handed downstream

- **T13** — the score, to dry-run against real schemes.
- **T16** — specificity and tally are **derived, never stored**; the ladder rung that produced each spoken name **is** stored.
- **T23** — a spoken answer costs 2 turns; this is an input to the confirmation ladder, not an output of it.
- **T18** — D6's widening order.
- **T11** — box roster D1 fixes which closed sets must exist.

## Rejected

- **Greedy information gain** — needs a caller prior we do not have; a uniform prior makes it a value-count proxy.
- **Keypad tie-break bonus** — subsumed by the turn divisor.
- **`state` always asked first** — replaced by the unasked-hard-box speaking rule.
- **Barred-vs-mismatched as the hard/soft axis** — left the ladder with one rung.
- **Per-cell hard/soft** (`age` hard on a pension, soft on a scholarship) — correct, but reopens closed T06 to add a column per facet. Not for 12 Sep.
- **Widening upward from 1 survivor** to reach the bar's "2".
- **Tally as survivor ordering** — mathematically flat across survivors.
