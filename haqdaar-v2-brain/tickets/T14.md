# T14 · The turn clock: silence, barge-in and call length

**Type:** `wayfinder:grilling` (HITL) · **Module:** Audio · **Blocked by:** ~~T01~~, ~~T03~~, ~~T24~~ · **Blocks:** ~~T15~~, ~~T17~~
**Status: CLOSED — 9 Sep 2026 · map rev 18**

## Question

What are the timings of a turn, and what happens when the caller doesn't behave?

## What the ticket got wrong before it was opened

Two of the five branches were mis-shaped rather than merely undecided, and one was already answered by a provider fact nobody had looked up.

- **"3 s per turn" never said from what to what.** A turn contains the caller's thinking time and our response gap, and only the second is a budget. Sizing them as one number guarantees the wrong answer whichever number you pick.
- **"Audio beats DTMF" was a taste ruling standing on an arithmetic error.** It silently repriced every keypad turn in the system.
- **Barge-in was written as *is it possible*.** It is. That was never the constraint.

## Branch 1 — The per-turn latency budget

**Verdict: two clocks. A 700 ms endpoint window, then 1.2 s of ours.**

The budget is on the gap from Sarvam's `vad.speech_end` to the first byte of our reply on the wire. Everything before `speech_end` belongs to the caller.

| Stage | Budget | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `transcript.final` after `speech_end` | 150–300 ms | Sarvam `stream_type="fast"`, sub-150 ms TTFT |
| Model (Gemini Flash-Lite, ~1.5k in / ~20 out) | 300–500 ms | T11's contract is three short fields |
| Engine | ~0 | T09 made a turn one AND instruction |
| Mouth | ~50 ms | T06 left no synthesis in the call path — cache read only |
| Network, Mumbai ↔ Plivo edge | 50–100 ms | Plivo routes to the edge nearest the caller |
| **Total** | **≤ 1.2 s** | felt gap ~1.9 s including the endpoint window |

**Sarvam configuration is `endpointing=vad`, `stream_type="fast"`, `silence_duration_ms=700`, `min_speech_duration_ms=250` (default, untouched).**

- **`stream_type="fast"` is not optional.** `balanced` buffers audio into ~1000 ms chunks before sending, and Sarvam's VAD cannot see end-of-speech until the chunk containing the silence arrives. That is a full second in front of every turn, for free, invisibly.
- **Plivo's ~20 ms media frames are forwarded unbatched.** The 500–1000 ms chunking penalty that dominates every published Sarvam latency budget is a client-library artifact. We write our own client, so we do not inherit it. **This is the largest single saving in the chain and it costs nothing.**
- **`silence_duration_ms=700` sits deliberately above the vendor's 250–500 ms guidance.** That guidance optimises felt latency for a fluent speaker. We are optimising against cutting off a hesitant one: a truncated utterance loses its span, T11's span guard drops the value, and the turn is spent for nothing. Dead air costs a second; a cut-off costs a turn. The direction is derived; the number is not — **named as a dial, and the cheapest one in the system**, since `silence_duration_ms` applies immediately on a live stream and can be tuned mid-demo. T13 is its first evidence.
- **`endpointing=vad`, not `manual`.** Manual would put the endpoint decision in our hands, which sounds like ownership and is actually a requirement to build a VAD on μ-law to replace one we are already paying for.

**When the budget is blown: model hard timeout at 2.0 s, counting as one of T11's two failures, with no in-turn retry.** Retrying inside a turn doubles the dead air to buy a second draw from the same distribution; T11 already decided the fallback is the keypad, not persistence.

**No filler or thinking tone.** At a 1.2 s budget it buys nothing, and it would add a fixed line × 3 to T15's inventory for the privilege.

## Branch 2 — Barge-in mechanism, and the policy re-opened

**Verdict: the mechanism is `clearAudio`. Speech barge-in resolves to *available* and is declined anyway.**

Mechanism, from Plivo's stream protocol:

- Out: `playAudio`, `checkpoint`, `clearAudio`. In: `start`, `media`, `dtmf`, `playedStream`, `clearedAudio`.
- On a `dtmf` event we send `clearAudio` immediately; `clearedAudio` acknowledges. **Overrun is ~200–300 ms of audio after the keypress** — one edge round trip. That is the correction latency T12's Door A read-back rides on, and it is fine.

**Rev 2 made speech barge-in conditional on the provider streaming live media. T01 delivered it and Sarvam emits `vad.speech_start`, so the condition resolves to yes — and it is still off for 12 Sep, on grounds rev 2 did not have.**

- The trigger would be a VAD verdict on 8 kHz household audio. A television, a bus, a family member in the room. Tunable, not solvable.
- A false fire costs the caller the question they needed to hear. A true fire saves ~2 s of a short prompt. **The asymmetry runs the wrong way and the corpus is noisy.**
- The one long line in the system — the read-back — was keypad-interruptible only under rev 2 already. So speech barge-in was never available where it would have mattered.

**Replaced by: listen always, interrupt only on keypad.** Sarvam hears the caller through the entire prompt. `audioTrack="inbound"` streams the caller leg only, so our own audio is never in what we transcribe. The caller who answers over the question loses nothing — their transcript is there and the turn resolves the moment the prompt's `playedStream` lands. **We simply decline to stop talking.**

Consequence, stated so nobody adds a `clearAudio` here by accident: **the turn closes on the later of the prompt's `playedStream` and `vad.speech_end`.** This is also what the queue does unaided — `playAudio` appends to a FIFO, so a reply sent early just queues behind the question.

Recorded as the dial: one flag, fire `clearAudio` on `vad.speech_start`. Same shape as T24's detection-as-hint.

## Branch 2b — The checkpoint (the ticket's real finding)

**Verdict: every line ships with a terminal `checkpoint`, and every timer in the system starts at its `playedStream`.**

Plivo's playback queue holds **~60 seconds of audio**. `playAudio` is fire-and-forget. **The moment we finish sending a line is not the moment the caller finishes hearing it**, and on a long read-back the two are half a minute apart. Any timer started locally at send is measuring our socket, not the caller's ear.

Two things fall straight out:

1. **T12's `t_name` stops being a prediction.** A second checkpoint sits immediately before the first word of the scheme name. Origin is `vad.speech_end` of the opener utterance; the stamp is that checkpoint's `playedStream`. **Both ends are provider events.** T12 predicted 3–5 s and now gets a measurement instead of a hope. `t_end` is the terminal checkpoint of `summary`, same mechanism.
2. **Nothing may hang up on a queued buffer.** Every terminal state in the system is: play the closing line → wait its `playedStream` → hang up. A hangup issued against a full queue means the caller hears nothing at all, which reads as a dropped call and fails T04.

Handed to T15 as an interface constraint: **Mouth is append-and-mark, never play-and-wait.**

## Branch 3 — The silence ladder

**Verdict: 6 s rungs, and rung 1 re-plays the question rather than asking whether anyone is there.**

The number is derived, not felt. T10 models a turn at ~8 s; our gap is ~1.9 s; so the caller's share of a normal turn is ~6 s. **A caller who has made no sound 6 s after the prompt's `playedStream` is outside the design's own model of a normal turn.** If T10's 8 moves, this moves with it.

| Rung | At | What plays | New audio? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +6 s from `playedStream` | **the question again** | no |
| 2 | +6 s from rung 1's `playedStream` | presence line — *still there? press any key* | **one fixed line × 3** |
| 3 | +6 s from rung 2's `playedStream` | the existing closing line, then hang up | no |

- **Rung 1 is a re-play, not a presence check.** On this corpus silence means *didn't follow*, not *walked away*. A presence line at rung 1 costs audio and answers a question nobody asked. The default's 7 s / 15 s ladder had the right count of rungs and the wrong first one.
- **~30 s of total grace** before hangup, including two re-plays. A caller who put the phone down gets a fair hearing.
- **Turn 0 runs a 4 s gap, not 6.** T24 ruled *two plays then default Hindi* and left the interval open. Choosing a language is a hearing question, not a recall question — there is nothing to think about, so 6 s is just dead air on the one turn every caller pays for.
- **No silence timer runs during a read-back.** The caller is not expected to speak. The only clock there is the ceiling.
- **The ladder's hangup is a terminal state** and writes a closing line like any other → handed to T18.

## Branch 4 — Maximum call length

**Verdict: 10 minutes, and it should never fire.**

Five minutes cuts off a legitimate call. Turn 0 (~20 s with a re-play) + 8 turns at ~8 s (~65 s) + two silence ladders (~36 s) + a read-back with keypad-offered sections (~90 s) + an *"anything else?"* round (another ~155 s) runs past six minutes without anything going wrong.

**Ten minutes is a safety net for a wedged socket or a handset in a pocket, not a design parameter.** Nothing in the designed path reaches it — that is the point. **A ceiling that fires during the 12 Sep test is a bug report, not a caller.**

- It also bounds the two unbounded things in the design: `*` re-pins and `#` repeats, both of which cost no turn and can be pressed forever. **This discharges T24's handoff that turn-0 events must be bounded by the wall clock rather than the turn counter** — the wall clock is here and it is 10 minutes.
- Ending: the existing closing line, then `playedStream`, then hang up via REST against the `callId` from the `start` event. **Adds no inventory to T15.**
- `streamTimeout="600"` set to match, as a backstop.

**`keepCallAlive="false"`.** A dead socket should end the call rather than strand a caller on a silent line. Both outcomes fail T04, but only one of them is cruel. Plivo's docs advise `true` — that advice is about calls dropping at connect for want of a following XML element, which does not describe a stream opened immediately and held for the whole call. **But it is a provider behaviour, not a design argument: marked ratify-on-hardware, the one T14 ruling that must be confirmed on the first real call.** If it does drop calls at connect, flip to `true` and let `streamTimeout` bound the strand.

## Branch 5 — Speech and DTMF in the same turn

**Verdict: the earlier ruling is overturned. The turn closes on the first *complete* input.**

Not *DTMF beats audio* — a sharper rule that justifies itself:

> A keypress is complete on arrival. Speech is complete only at endpoint.

**The overturn is arithmetic, not taste.** DTMF arrives on the same socket at keypress time; a transcript arrives only after the 700 ms endpoint window plus finalisation. So *audio wins* requires **holding every keypress** for the endpoint window to see whether speech follows. That makes T10's *keypad costs 1 turn, speech costs 2* false — keypad would pay the same wall clock as speech on **every turn of every call**, and the keypad preference that T10, T11, T18 and T24 all lean on would be a fiction.

Handling:

- On `dtmf`, send Sarvam `{"event":"flush"}` to force-finalise the buffered audio, act on the digit, and **write the finalised text as `discarded_transcript` on the turn's LOG line.** The discard becomes evidenced rather than silent — T16's own reason for keeping the full transcript.
- **`*` and `#` are control keys.** Never answers, never in a menu, never close a turn. Digits 0–9 are answers or menu selections and do close it. This partition is what keeps T24's `*` from ever being eaten as a box value.

## Inherited item — `#`

**Verdict: `#` repeats the last thing the system said.**

The keypad twin of T11's `REPEAT` class. It cannot be misheard, costs no model call, consumes no turn, and hands the caller rung 1 of the silence ladder on demand.

- **It is what makes *listen always, interrupt only on keypad* affordable.** The main cost of not interrupting is a caller who missed something; `#` gives them the fix directly.
- Mechanically: `clearAudio`, then re-push the same file with a fresh checkpoint.
- **During a read-back, `#` re-reads in the same language and writes a second `t_name`** — exactly as T24's `*` re-reads after re-pinning. No collision: `*` changes the language and re-reads, `#` re-reads.
- **At turn 0, `#` re-plays the greeting and does not consume one of T24's two plays**, or a curious caller would be defaulted into Hindi for pressing a key twice. Bounded by the wall clock, per branch 4.

## Inherited item — T16's turn-count agreement

**Verdict: discharged by a provider event rather than a convention.**

| Condition | Class | Consumes a cap turn? | T11's two-non-ANSWER counter? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vad.speech_start` fired, final empty or span-less | **NOISE** | yes | yes |
| no `vad.speech_start` in the window | **SILENCE** | **no** | no |

A SILENCE line carries the current `turn_n` unchanged plus a `silence_n` of 1 or 2, on T24's precedent that the cap is a predicate on a monotonic counter rather than a claim that every line is a cap turn. The turn is still open; the same question is still unanswered.

**So the silent caller is bounded by the ladder and the noisy caller by T11's keypad drop** — two different failures, two different bounds, neither borrowing the other's.

## Handoffs

- **T15** — inventory is **3N + 2**; the checkpoint contract; the scheme name must be its own file; Mouth's interface is append-and-mark over a ~60 s FIFO.
- **T17** — the Audio module's outward interface: `playedStream` as the completion signal, first-complete-input as the turn boundary, the `dtmf`/`clearAudio` pair.
- **T12 / T12b** — `t_name` origin and stamp pinned; the 3–5 s prediction now has an instrument.
- **T16** — `discarded_transcript` added to the line schema; NOISE/SILENCE settled mechanically.
- **T18** — the silence ladder's hangup is a terminal state and writes a closing line.
- **T23** — `#` is taken, so both control keys are spoken for; the wording work has no key left to claim.
- **T13** — `silence_duration_ms=700` and the 1.2 s gap are predictions on record, and T10's ~8 s/turn model gets its first check.
- **T05 / T19** — ratify `keepCallAlive="false"` on the first real call.

## Left open

- The **700 ms endpoint window** has a direction, not a derivation.
- **`keepCallAlive="false"`** is provider behaviour, not design — ratify on hardware.
- **Speech barge-in** is designed, costed and switched off. One flag.
