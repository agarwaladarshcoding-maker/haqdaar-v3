# T07 · Build the corpus ingest pass

**Type:** `wayfinder:task` (AFK) · **Module:** Data
**Blocked by:** ~~T06~~ (closed 29 Aug), ~~T22~~ (closed 10 Sep) — **fully unblocked as of 10 Sep, MAP rev 21**
**Blocks:** T12b, T13 · *(the original "blocks T12" was wrong twice over: T12 closed 4 Sep, and the dependency ran the other way — T12 handed T07 the alias contract)*

**Retyped 10 Sep (rev 22).** The ticket's output is no longer a corpus. It is **the pass that builds one**, and its acceptance test is that N is a parameter. Everything below follows from that.

## Question

Nothing to decide. Build the pass that turns the raw scrape into structured records in the T06 shape, cutting the `occupation` vocabulary out of the same eligibility prose while it is open.

**N is a build parameter, not a design decision.** T04 (rev 7) already put the production scheme count out of the 12 Sep bar entirely. Rev 22 goes further: **no clause of this ticket may scale with N in human time.** 50, 100 or 5,000 must cost the same number of people, which is zero. The title's old "50" is retired; it was a charting-day number and was never a target.

**Done when** the pass runs end to end on the scrape, unattended, and emits a numbered snapshot in which every row passes all three build gates, every facet column holds a closed-set value or `ANY`, every row carries a source URL and a `facets_source` stamp, the excluded rows are listed with the clause that dropped them, and the `occupation` value list exists in three languages with its count recorded.

## The human is out of the loop, everywhere

**Rev 22 amendment, and it is the load-bearing one.** The previous body put a person in three places: a human read of every scheme's eligibility prose, human-authored `summary` lines in three languages, and verifier initials per row. All three are removed. **No step in the ingest path waits on a person.** The only human in HAQDAAR is the caller.

| Was | Is now |
|---|---|
| Human reads eligibility prose, confirms each `ANY` is real silence (`facets_verified`) | Model derives facets against the closed set; row carries **`facets_source`** — `derived` today, `audited` if anyone ever audits it |
| `summary` authored natively per language, ~35 words | **Generated in English at ingest, capped at ~35 words, then translated** to Hindi and Marathi like every other outbound string |
| Verifier initials per row | Dropped. Provenance is the snapshot id, the scrape timestamp and the source URL |
| Third-party dataset spot-check by a human (T02) | Machine assertions at ingest — see *Fabrication* below |

`facets_verified` as T06 tightened it — *a human read this and confirmed the silence* — **has no holder and is retired as a gate.** It survives only as a value of `facets_source`, so a later audit has somewhere to write its result. **T21 owns the audit** and may sample rather than census; nothing in T07 waits for it.

**What this costs, recorded rather than absorbed.** A derived facet can be wrong in a way a read facet cannot: prose that says *"preference shall be given to women"* is not the same as *"for women"*, and the model may flatten it. This sits on the same shelf as rev 11's suspended truth lock and T16's persisted transcript — an accepted exposure, named, with a ticket over it (T21). It is not a defect discovered later.

**The generated `summary` is the same trade, already permitted.** Rev 11 suspended the truth lock for 12 Sep: no human verifies a translated string before it is spoken. Native authoring was a quality choice made when N was assumed small, and it is not compatible with "any number of schemes." The English hop makes the system's *thinking* language-free; it does not make its *speech* language-free, and speech is per-language and per-scheme. Machines do that part.

## The three build gates — all machine-checkable

T06 chose a build gate over a second sentinel: **a row that cannot be fully expressed does not enter the snapshot at all.** Two more arrived in the same shape. A scheme fails the snapshot if any of these is true, and **each one is now decidable by code with nothing to ask anyone:**

1. **Expressibility (T06, rev 5; machine-checked from rev 22).** Any facet column that does not resolve to a closed-set value or `ANY`. `ANY` means *the prose is silent on this box* — now a model judgement recorded in `facets_source`, not a confirmed human read. `ANY` is the only sentinel; there is no record-level UNKNOWN. *(UNKNOWN exists at runtime as a reserved value in every box's set — T11 — but it describes a caller, never a scheme.)*
2. **Alias floor (T12, rev 15; amended T24, rev 17).** Fewer than **3 aliases per language**, stored as spoken. At least one alias per **non-English** language must be the **code-mixed** form — scheme names are proper nouns nobody translates, and three pure-Marathi aliases for *PM-KISAN* are three strings no caller will ever say. **Aliases are generated at ingest**, code-mixed forms included; the count is a `len()`.
3. **Read-back completeness (T15, rev 19).** Any of the **six read-back chunks** missing, or present but not rendered, in **any** of the three languages. Checked against the audio manifest — 18 entries per row, present or not. A scheme the system cannot finish speaking does not enter the snapshot.

**Uniqueness is a build-time gate, not authored code.** An alias appearing on **≥3 schemes** is a category word and is dropped from all of them. An alias on **exactly 2** *is* the disambiguation pair — which is where Door A's cap of two candidates comes from (T12). No ambiguity list is ever written by hand; it falls out of the gate.

**Run the gates as a pre-filter, not a post-check.** Order them by cost: alias floor first (cheapest, excludes most), expressibility second, read-back completeness last, because it is the only one that spends TTS. Discovering at the end of a render batch that a row was never admissible is the expensive failure.

**The floor of three is the named dial** (map, *Not yet specified*): it was set from feel, and a wrong floor silently shrinks the corpus. It lives in `contracts/tunables.py` (T17) and moves with no ceremony. If it bites, **lower the floor rather than admit a scheme the caller cannot name.** T12b is the first evidence it will get.

## Nothing in the pass scales with N in human time

The acceptance test for rev 22. Every step is batch, parallel, and content-addressed so re-runs are free:

- **Facet derivation** — one model call per scheme over its eligibility prose, closed set enforced in code after the return (T11's rule, unchanged).
- **Alias generation** — one call per scheme per language; uniqueness resolved over the whole table afterwards.
- **`summary`** — generated once in English, translated twice.
- **Translation** — the four verbatim myScheme sections plus exclusions plus `summary`, per T08: pre-translated and cached at ingest, never generated live.
- **Rendering** — 18 clips per row, batch TTS at ingest. TTS is not in the runtime at all (T15) and the runtime holds no TTS credentials.
- **Mask table** — rebuilt at build time from the facet columns, as T06 already specified.

**Storage does not cap N and never did.** 8 kHz μ-law is 8 KB/s; a 20 s clip is ~160 KB; N=100 is ~290 MB, N=1,000 is ~3 GB, both served from local disk. T19's "~200 MB" is a sizing footnote for 12 Sep, **not a ceiling**, and rev 22 corrects any reading of it as one.

**Fabrication is handled by assertion, not by eye.** T02's warning stands — at least one public third-party myScheme dataset fabricates eligibility rules and Hindi text on fetch failure — but the machine checks now carry it: the scrape must come from myScheme directly, every row must carry its four section anchors non-empty, and rows failing that drop out with a logged reason. **The fabricated-Hindi risk disappears by construction**: rev 22 generates and translates our own outbound text, so myScheme's own Hindi and Marathi content is never read back and never enters the snapshot.

## The occupation cut is a step in this pass, not a phase in front of it

T22 was going to hand T07 an occupation value list recovered from the myScheme wizard. It closed 10 Sep by narrowing instead: the wizard's rail is the retired 18-box one, and the portal was never going to be authoritative anyway, because T02 established that **eligibility is prose and every filter value is ours to derive at ingest.** The cut moves inside T07 — the ingest pass already opens every scheme's eligibility prose, and doing it first as a separate pass reads the prose twice.

### The rule the list must satisfy

**A value earns a place only if at least one scheme in the corpus discriminates on it.** Not the occupations Indian citizens have — the occupations *these schemes distinguish between*. Two consequences:

- The list is **closed over the corpus by construction**, which is what gate 1 wants and what T08's offline test base wants (~150–200 keyword values per language, frozen, testable against a table of utterances).
- The list **grows with the corpus, and that is correct, not drift.** The mask table is rebuilt at build time anyway (T06), so this costs a rebuild and nothing else.

### Cardinality is a design decision, not an outcome

**T15 (rev 19): a box with more than nine values cannot be keypad-dropped.** `occupation` is a **soft** box (T10) sitting third in the widening ladder, so it is one of the boxes most likely to be reached by the fallback path with a caller who has already failed twice on it.

- **At ≤9 values**, `occupation` keeps its keypad menu and T11's two-non-ANSWER drop works normally.
- **Above 9**, it falls straight to **UNKNOWN**, and T09's widening takes it from there — safe, but it costs a box the ladder was counting on.

**Cut for ≤9 if the corpus allows it.** If the honest count runs over, **record the number and the reason rather than trimming to fit**, and note in the resolution that `occupation` has lost its keypad form. **At large N this will run over** — the cut is over what the corpus discriminates on, and more schemes discriminate more finely. That is the expected outcome, not a failure; the demo snapshot is where ≤9 is worth trying for.

### Three languages, same rule as every other keyword

The list is part of T08's keyword list, so it ships in all three languages: **one string per language, one audio file per value** (T15) — never a carrier sentence with a slot dropped in, because a joined-in inflection error in Hindi or Marathi reads as broken. Machine translation, truth lock suspended (rev 11), debt recorded.

**Code-mixing applies here as it does to aliases**, for the opposite reason. *Farmer*, *student*, *labourer* have real Hindi and Marathi forms callers actually use — but the English word is often spoken inside a Marathi sentence anyway. The span guard compares the span against the **transcript**, not against a vocabulary (T11), so the runtime is already handled; but the **utterance table for the offline test base must contain the code-mixed forms**, or the test base tests a caller who does not exist.

### What was lost, and where it is recorded

We can no longer say the occupation vocabulary came from the portal. That is a **provenance cost, not a correctness one**, and [T21](T21.md) owns it. If anyone later runs the optional 20-minute wizard errand, the portal list is a **cross-check**: a divergence is evidence for T21, not a defect in T07.

## What the pass produces per row

Per T06's five field groups — identity/provenance, matching, filtration, negative rules, read-back:

- **Facets** derived from the eligibility prose, each a closed-set value or `ANY`. Numerics stored **exact**; banding happens at build, never at capture.
- **Box 0 `category`**, read off the opening sentence. Load-bearing: applied before question one, costs zero questions.
- **Exclusions** subtracted from a column's admitted set where they can be. Where they cannot, they become **`gate_notes`** — a list, never free text, never spoken, never filtered on, carried into the LOG as a declared blind spot.
- **Aliases**, ≥3 per language, stored as spoken, ≥1 code-mixed per non-English language, generated at ingest.
- **Six read-back chunks**: the four verbatim myScheme sections (pre-separated by section anchor, T02), plus exclusions, plus **`summary`** — ~35 words, generated in English and translated, because the bar wants a named scheme back inside 20 s and verbatim `benefit_text` is not 20 s in Marathi.
- **`facets_source`** — `derived` or `audited`. **Source URL and scrape timestamp.** No initials.

## The fixture is part of this ticket

**T17 (rev 20) makes Data's owner the curator**, because the fixture is a miniature snapshot and any other curator guarantees shape drift. **Five chosen schemes, not three arbitrary ones**: match / soft-miss / hard-miss / near-`ANY` generic / alias-sharing pair — three arbitrary schemes cannot exercise the hard-miss vs soft-miss distinction the whole fallback rests on. Plus three personas.

**The fixture *is* T08's offline test base — one artifact, not two.** Its ~10 audio stubs carry real durations, which is what makes the turn clock testable before a phone line exists. **Curating five rows out of a built snapshot is selection, not authoring** — it is the one place a person picks something, and it costs minutes at any N.

## Record on resolution

- **Wall-clock and cost for one full pass at the N it was run on**, and the per-row marginal cost. That number, not a human verification rate, is what sizes the corpus from here.
- **The `occupation` value count**, and whether it came in at ≤9 or over — and if over, the reason, and the note that `occupation` lost its keypad form.
- **How many schemes the three gates excluded, and on which clause.** If the alias floor is doing most of the excluding, that is the evidence for lowering it.
- **The facet-derivation prompt and closed sets as they stood**, so T21's audit has something to audit against.

The map's open question *"who eventually verifies spoken content, and at what rate"* is **no longer waiting on T07** — rev 22 removes the verifier from the path entirely and hands the question to T21 as a sampling problem.
