# T17 · Module boundaries and frozen interfaces

**Type:** `wayfinder:grilling` (HITL) · **Module:** cross · **Blocked by:** ~~T09~~, ~~T11~~, ~~T14~~, ~~T15~~, ~~T16~~, ~~T24~~, ~~T23 (edge cut — see below)~~ · **Closed 9 Sep 2026**

## Question

The destination gate. What are the four modules, what is the exact signature of every call across their boundaries, and who owns each one?

To resolve:

- The four module boundaries ratified against what the earlier tickets actually decided.
- Every cross-module function signature, frozen: input type, output type, error behaviour.
- Repo layout, and how two people work without colliding.
- What each owner receives on day one: their module's contract and a test they must pass. **Fakes are already decided** (map rev 3): each owner writes a fake of their *own* interface as their first commit, from one shared fixture file — nobody writes fakes for dependencies. What is left here is the fixture's contents and who curates it.
- How the 12 documentation segments get re-cut to match module boundaries instead of narrative ones.
- What is deliberately NOT frozen, and who may change it without asking.

## Why it is last

Everything upstream feeds it. When this closes, the design is handoff-ready and the map is done.

# Resolution

**Headline: the roster survives, one module shrinks, and one module was never on the map at all.** T17 was framed as a ratification ticket. It is not — the nine-stage grouping hid an unowned orchestrator, and assigning it is the ticket's largest single act.

## 0 · The T23 block, cut rather than waited on

T23's remaining scope is **wording**: ~29 fixed lines × 3 languages, the rephrase form T11 implied and T15 surfaced, the `cardinality > 9 → UNKNOWN` phrasing, and the yes/no capture for echo-confirm. Every one of those is *content behind a line id*, in a table T15 already addressed and content-addressed.

T17 freezes the **id space**; T23 fills it. The dependency ran backwards, exactly as T12→T07 did. **The blocking edge is cut, not satisfied.**

One design consequence, and it is load-bearing rather than bookkeeping: **line ids are strings resolved through the manifest, never an enum with 29 members.** T15 pinned N ≈ 29 from an enumeration, not a derivation; if T23's authoring moves it to 27 or 34, nothing recompiles and no interface changes.

## 1 · The four modules, ratified — and the fifth thing

Checked clause by clause against what the tickets decided, not against the charting-day grouping.

**Audio** — Telephony + Mouth + Ear. Everything that touches a socket: Plivo WSS and DTMF (T01), `playAudio`/`checkpoint`/`playedStream` and both clocks (T14), Sarvam WSS with `vad` events, `flush` and `config.update` (T03, T24), `say`/`repeat`/`clear` over marks (T15). **Ratified**, with T15's carve-out held explicitly: the renderer is a **Data build tool wearing Mouth's name**, and Audio owns only what runs during a call.

**Engine** — Checker/Filter/Planner. T09's retained masks, T10's minimax selection, three stops and the widening ladder. **Ratified and enlarged** (see below).

**Data** — Corpus + LOG. Build-time half (scrape → row → gates → snapshot → render) and runtime half (read the snapshot, append the trace). **Ratified.** The pairing looked arbitrary and is not: T16 made the trace unreadable without the snapshot id and box vector, so LOG and Corpus share a reader and splitting them would split that reader.

**Model** — charted as **Router + Reader**. **Reader no longer exists.** T15 removed live rendering from the runtime and T06 made read-back six per-scheme chunks addressed by id; nothing composes read-back text while a caller is on the line, so there is no Reader to own. **Model is Router only** — the opener call and one call per question turn, plus the validation that stands in front of both.

### The unowned orchestrator

Something receives an Ear event, decides NOISE vs SILENCE vs keypress, calls Model, calls Engine, calls Mouth, writes the LOG line, counts turns against the cap, runs the silence ladder, and holds the box vector. **The nine stages never named it and no ticket ever assigned it.** It was invisible because every ticket resolved a *decision* the loop makes, and none resolved the loop.

Three placements were considered:

- **A fifth module** — rejected. Five owners against a four-owner premise, and the loop is not independently testable: it is nothing but the joins between the other four.
- **Audio** — rejected, and this is the near miss. Audio already owns the turn *clock*, and a turn is a clock construct (opens at a prompt's `playedStream`, closes on first complete input). But the loop's state is the box vector, and putting the box vector inside the language-pinned module makes the language-blind Engine a subroutine of it — **inverting the dependency T09 established on purpose.**
- **Engine** — **taken.** The loop's three pieces of state — box vector, turn counter, ladder rung — are already Engine's state, and are exactly what a T16 line is made of. Nothing moves; something previously nameless acquires a name.

**Price paid honestly:** T09's "the Engine is pure code" narrows to **Planner and Filter are pure; the loop is not.** The purity that mattered (T13's dry-run, T09's language-blindness) is preserved by keeping the pure core callable without Audio, Model or Log — see §2.

**Knock-on, and the best thing in this ticket:** because Engine owns the loop, **Audio's outward interface collapses to one settled turn.** Audio absorbs the 700 ms endpoint window, the DTMF-vs-speech race, the `flush` and `discarded_transcript`, and the 10-minute ceiling, and surfaces one `Input`. That is T14's *"the turn closes on the first complete input"* expressed as a signature rather than as a rule two people have to remember.

**Module graph:** `Data ← Model ← Engine`, `Audio ← Engine`. **Four modules, three edges, no cycles, and Audio depends on nothing.**

## 2 · The frozen signatures

**Language frozen with them, because a signature needs one: Python 3.11, asyncio, one process, one repo.** Not a preference — Plivo and Sarvam are both raw WSS, the whole corpus is ~200 MB in RAM (T15), and T19 has not chosen a host, so a single process with no external services is the only shape that survives every remaining answer.

### Data — build-time (an artifact contract, not a call)

```
snapshots/<snapshot_id>/
  schemes.jsonl      one flat row per scheme (T06, five field groups)
  masks.bin          packed (box, value) -> word
  vocab.json         closed value sets + code map (T09, T22)
  templates.json     line_id -> {lang -> render_key}, values expanded (T15, T23)
  manifest.json      the snapshot IS the manifest (T15)
audio/<render_key>.ulaw    one flat pool, shared across snapshots
```

`render_key = sha256(text ‖ lang ‖ voice_id ‖ tts_model ‖ 8000)` — frozen verbatim from T15.

**Three build gates. A snapshot failing any of them never flips the pointer:**
1. every facet expressible against the closed set, no second sentinel (T06);
2. alias floor — ≥3 per language, ≥1 code-mixed per non-English language, uniqueness gate applied (T12, T24);
3. all six read-back chunks rendered in all three languages (T15).

### Data — runtime · `Corpus`

```python
Corpus.load(snapshot_id)                  -> Corpus
Corpus.snapshot_id                        -> str
Corpus.mask(box, value)                   -> int
Corpus.values(box)                        -> tuple[ValueCode, ...]
Corpus.specificity(scheme_ix)             -> int
Corpus.scheme_id(scheme_ix)               -> str
Corpus.alias_lookup(text, lang)           -> tuple[str, ...]            # <=2, by the uniqueness gate
Corpus.alias_set(lang)                    -> Mapping[str, tuple[str, ...]]
Corpus.audio(line_id, lang, value=None)   -> RenderKey
Corpus.chunks(scheme_id, lang)            -> tuple[RenderKey, ...]      # 6, ordered
Corpus.gate_notes(scheme_id)              -> tuple[str, ...]
```

**Error behaviour, frozen: `Corpus` raises at `load` and never during a call.** Every runtime read is total, because the build gate already guaranteed it. A runtime `KeyError` would mean the gate is broken, and deploy is the right place to learn that — not turn four of a judged call.

### Data — runtime · `Log`

```python
Log.open(call_id, snapshot_id)                          -> Log
Log.write(line: dict)                                   -> None
Log.close(reason: str, ladder_rung: int | None = None,
          mode: str | None = None)                      -> None
```

**Amended 13 Sep 2026 — `Log.close` carries `ladder_rung` and `mode`.** The first freeze had `Log.close(reason: str)`. That cannot write the closing line T16 defines: `CallCloseRecord` holds `stop`, `ladder_rung` and `mode`, and build-plan Step 6's Done-when requires *"a closing line carrying `stop` and `ladder_rung`"*. Only the Engine knows the rung it reached and whether the call went keypad-only, and `close` is the last moment it can say so. The two new arguments are optional, so `Log.close(reason)` still works exactly as frozen. Same pattern as `Log.open`, which takes optional keyword extras (`caller_hash`, `lang`, `lang_source`, `logs_dir`, `t0`) behind the frozen positional pair: **the frozen call form is unchanged; nothing that used it breaks.**

**Error behaviour, frozen: a LOG write never kills a call.** A line failing T16 schema validation is written anyway, carrying `invalid: true`, with a warning to stderr. T04 judges pass/fail off the LOG alone — **a malformed line is still evidence; a missing line is nothing.** This is the one place in the design where we deliberately persist something known-wrong.

### Model

```python
Model(corpus)
Model.opener(transcript, lang)                  -> list[Stamp] | Unclear
Model.turn(transcript, box, window, ask_count)  -> Answer | Clarify | Repeat | Meta | Unclear
Model.failures                                  -> int
```

`Stamp = (box | "scheme", value, span)` — the opener returns a list (T11), including the `scheme` pseudo-box (T12); every question turn returns exactly one result. `window` is T11's bounded two-turn history; `ask_count` is the per-box rephrase counter.

**Error behaviour, frozen: Model never raises and never returns a value outside the closed set.** Hard timeout 2.0 s, **no in-turn retry** (T14), timeout returns `Unclear(reason="timeout")` and increments `failures`; Engine reads `failures` for T11's two-strikes-to-keypad-only.

**The span guard is Model's, not Engine's.** Model is the only module holding the transcript and the candidate value at the same moment. Closed-set validation and span validation both run *inside* Model before it returns, so **Engine is structurally incapable of receiving an unvalidated value.** T11's guard stops being a discipline and becomes a type boundary.

### Audio

```python
Audio.connect(ws)                -> Audio
Audio.select_language()          -> (Lang, LangSource)      # turn 0; two plays then default hi (T24)
Audio.say(sequence)              -> None                    # append-only, returns immediately (T15)
Audio.repeat()                   -> None                    # re-fires marks
Audio.clear()                    -> None
Audio.on_mark(mark)              -> awaitable[timestamp]    # playedStream, the ONLY completion signal
Audio.next_input(profile)        -> Digit | Speech | Noise | Silence(n) | Hangup
Audio.language                   -> Lang                    # settable; `*` re-pins Sarvam via config.update
Audio.hangup()                   -> None
```

`Speech` carries `text` and, where T14's flush fired, `discarded_transcript`. `profile` selects the silence gap: 4 s at turn 0, 6 s thereafter, no timer during read-back (T14).

**Audio never terminates a call.** The silence ladder surfaces `Silence(1|2|3)` and Engine decides: rung 1 → `repeat()`, rung 2 → the presence line, rung 3 → the closing line, await its mark, then `hangup()`. This costs three trips through an in-process loop instead of one, and buys three things: Audio needs no corpus knowledge, **every SILENCE line T16 requires is written where every other line is written**, and the ladder becomes three Engine branches a test can drive without a socket.

`Audio.hangup()` plays nothing. T14's rule — *play the closing line, await its `playedStream`, then hang up* — is Engine's to obey, because Engine is the module that knows which closing line.

### Engine

```python
Engine.run_call(audio, model, corpus, log) -> None          # the loop

Planner.next_action(box_vector, corpus)    -> Ask(box) | Widen(box) | Stop(reason)
Filter.survivors(box_vector, corpus)       -> tuple[int, ...]
Filter.tally(box_vector, corpus)           -> Mapping[int, int]
Filter.miss_set(box_vector, corpus, ix)    -> frozenset[BoxId]
```

**`Planner` and `Filter` take no Audio, no Model and no Log.** This is the seam that keeps T09's purity claim honest after the loop moved in, and it is what makes **T13's dry-run an afternoon rather than a week** — the narrowing can be exercised over the utterance table with three of four modules absent.

**Engine assembles the LOG line; Data persists it.** Engine holds every field value (T16's schema is made of Engine state plus Audio's transcript plus Model's class/value/span); Data owns the file, the schema and the flush discipline. The schema lives in one module, as T16 intended.

## 3 · Repo layout, and collision as a solved problem

```
haqdaar/
  audio/          owner A
  model/          owner B
  engine/         owner C
  data/           owner D      build/ | runtime/
  contracts/      NO OWNER
  fixtures/
  tests/
```

**Collisions are a file-ownership problem, not a branch-discipline problem.** Every shared type from §2 — `BoxId`, `ValueCode`, `Lang`, `LangSource`, `Stamp`, `Input`, `TurnResult`, `RenderKey`, and the T16 line schema — lives in `contracts/`, owned by nobody. Once they are extracted, **no two owners ever need to edit the same file**, and merge conflict stops being managed and starts being impossible.

Two rules:
- A PR touching two module directories is wrong by construction and gets split.
- A PR touching `contracts/` needs the owners on **both sides of the changed edge**. Adarsh breaks ties.

**If the team is fewer than four**, merge **Data+Engine** and **Audio+Model**: the first pair is offline and pure and shares the snapshot as its whole world; the second pair is socket- and API-bound and shares latency as its whole problem. Merging across that line instead gives one person both clocks and neither of the things they time.

## 4 · The fixture, and the day-one tests

**Curator: Data's owner.** The fixture is a miniature snapshot and Data's build already emits snapshots. Any other curator guarantees the fixture and the real snapshot drift in shape, and the drift shows up as four passing fakes and one failing integration.

**"One persona, three schemes" (map rev 3) is too thin, and specifically so:** three arbitrary schemes cannot exercise T09's hard-miss/soft-miss distinction, on which the entire fallback ladder and T04's honest-dead-end pass both rest. The schemes must be **chosen for what they break**, not chosen for being three.

**Five schemes:**

| | role | proves |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | exact match on the persona | the happy path and the ≤4 stop |
| S2 | soft-miss on `income_band` | speakable-with-a-label; T09's miss-set |
| S3 | hard-miss on `state` | never-speakable; the speaking rule (T10) |
| S4 | `ANY` on five of seven boxes | specificity ordering beats tally (T10) |
| S5 | shares one alias with S1 | Door A's cap of 2 and the disambiguation turn (T12) |

**Three personas:** **P1** narrows to 1 survivor (happy path); **P2** narrows to 0 and runs the full widening ladder to a labelled nearest-two (T04's honest dead-end, which is a **pass**); **P3** names a scheme at the opener (Door A).

**Nine utterances** — one per persona per language. **The fixture and T08's offline test base are the same artifact.** Do not build two: T13's dry-run, T12b's measurement and T11's three-way context bake-off all then run against the thing that already exists on day one.

**~10 audio stubs** — correct-duration silence, named by render key — so Audio's fake plays something with real timing and **the turn clock is testable before a phone line exists.** This is what makes T05's lateness survivable.

**Day-one test, one per owner, each runnable with the other three faked:**

- **Audio** — play a three-file sequence with a terminal mark, take a DTMF mid-play, return `Digit` carrying `discarded_transcript`, report a `playedStream` timestamp.
- **Model** — return the expected stamps for the nine utterances; drop the one whose span is not in its transcript.
- **Engine** — run P1 to a stop in ≤4 turns, and P2 through the full ladder to two nearest schemes labelled as non-matches.
- **Data** — load the fixture, answer `mask` / `specificity` / `chunks`, and **reject** a sixth scheme missing its Marathi `summary` at the build gate.

**The four tests together are the integration test.** `make demo-fixture` runs a whole call against four fakes and prints the LOG. That is map rev 3's stated purpose — *interfaces fail on day one instead of day nine* — delivered as one command rather than as an intention.

## 5 · The 12 segments: retired, not re-cut

**Verdict: do not re-cut them.**

The map demoted them to evidence on 22 Aug. Everything in them either came back through a ticket or did not. A re-cut segment is a **fourth document restating the map at a different resolution**, stale at the next revision, and directly against the map's own rule that a decision lives in exactly one place. Re-cutting manufactures the second place.

What replaces them: **four module briefs, ~2 pages each, generated from the map**, each containing only the owner's signature block, the ticket links behind each clause, the day-one test, and the not-frozen list from §6. Nothing authored beside the map; everything zoomed from it.

Anything in the 12 segments that never came back through a ticket is **fog**, and goes to *Not yet specified* — not into a brief.

## 6 · What is frozen and what is not

**Frozen — changing it needs the owners on both sides of the edge, plus a map revision:**
- the four signature blocks in §2
- the T16 per-turn line schema
- the snapshot layout, the manifest, and the render key formula
- the seven-box roster and the *identity* of each closed set
- the five model classes and the three fields

**Not frozen — the owner changes it without asking anyone:**
- everything inside a module
- the **contents** of the closed value sets (T22 is open; Data changes them daily)
- the wording and translation of every fixed line and every question form (T23 — this is precisely why T23 does not block)
- voice id and TTS vendor (T15's render key made a swap a re-render, not a redesign)
- prompt text inside Model, so long as the output contract holds
- the widening ladder's order and the greeting's clause order — both already on record as reversible at zero cost

**Every tunable in one unowned `contracts/tunables.py`, changeable by anyone, no ceremony and no map revision:** 700 ms endpoint window, 1.2 s response budget, 6/6/6 silence rungs, 4 s turn-0 gap, 2.0 s model timeout, 8-turn cap, alias floor of 3, ≤4 survivor stop, 10-minute ceiling. **A number that needs a ceremony will not be changed mid-demo**, and T14 explicitly wants `silence_duration_ms` tuned on a live stream.

**Seam disputes: the fixture test is the arbiter.** Whoever's day-one test still passes is not the one holding the bug. No adjudication, no meeting.

## What this ticket surfaced

- **The orchestrator was never charted.** T17 assigned it rather than ratifying it — the one place this ticket did more than its title.
- **`contracts/` needs an arbiter of last resort.** Adarsh, by default; recorded in Notes, not ticketed.
- **T23's scope is now sharper than the map had it**: it is the authoring of the template table's contents against an id space T17 froze — and the id space tolerates its count moving.
- **T05 is four days past its latest safe start.** Not T17's business, but T17 is the last design ticket, and nothing else is now standing between the design and a phone line that does not yet exist.
