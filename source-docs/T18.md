# ~~T18 · The fallback ladder: nothing fits, or nothing works~~

**Type:** `wayfinder:grilling` (HITL) · **Module:** cross · **Blocked by:** ~~T09~~, ~~T10~~, ~~T11~~ · **Closed 10 Sep 2026**

## Question

The policy is decided (see map, rev 2). This ticket turns it into wording and thresholds someone can implement.

To resolve:

- **Zero survivors.** Which mask gets widened on the retry, and how do we choose it? What if widening produces 40 survivors?
- **"Nearest two, not matches."** What sentence says that without ever implying eligibility? This wording is a lock candidate — it is the exact place the never-mislead rule is most likely to break.
- **Keypad-only mode.** What triggers the drop: model timeout, ASR failure, provider error? How many failures before dropping? Is the caller told, and in what words?
- Can keypad-only mode complete a whole call end to end, or does it only survive long enough to reach a read-back? (If Door B needs no model once boxes can be keyed in, it can complete — confirm against T09.)
- Does the call ever hang up on its own, and what is the last thing the caller hears?

## Why it matters

Every failure path is a place the system can say something untrue to a citizen. These are the sentences a demo never rehearses and a judge always finds.

## Resolution

**Two corrections to the map before anything else.** Rev 21's closing line — *"nothing remains to decide before someone goes and builds"* — lists T05, T07, T23 and the two prototypes and omits T18 and T21. T18 is not an errand; it decides the sentences where the never-mislead rule is most likely to break and it moves T15's inventory by ~30%. And **T18's own first branch was half-stale**: *"which mask gets widened, and how do we choose it"* was answered by T09 (rev 12) and T10 (rev 13), and T18's stated default — *widen the most recently applied mask* — is the exact clause rev 2 already superseded. What survived branch one was *when the ladder stops* and *what happens at forty*.

### 1 · Zero survivors

- **Zero survivors is a fourth stop condition, named separately in the LOG.** T10 listed three. An empty survivor set does technically trip the zero-information stop — nothing splits an empty set — but by accident, and a reader must be able to tell *nothing was left* from *no question helped*. Questioning halts the instant survivors reach zero: AND against an empty set stays empty, so no further question can recover it. This holds at any turn, including the opener.
- **Fixed order, no adaptivity.** T10's `income_band → age → occupation` is a **trust** order — least-trusted first — not an efficiency one. Dropping whichever box happens to release the most schemes is an efficiency argument beating a correctness argument, and it makes `ladder_rung` unreadable without re-running the algorithm, which is the thing T16 refuses.
- **The ladder stops at the first rung producing ≥1 survivor.** Every rung discards a fact the caller stated; discarding more than necessary is a larger misstatement of what was matched on.
- **Rungs over unanswered soft boxes are skipped, not counted.** A box that is UNASKED or UNKNOWN appended no mask (T09), so there is nothing to widen. At most 4 rungs exist and typically 1–2 run.
- **`ladder_rung` stays a single integer k** and the dropped set derives from k plus the box vector plus the fixed order. The ladder is monotone, so the integer reconstructs the set. No line-schema change; derive-don't-store holds and T16's exception is not widened.
- **Forty survivors answers itself, because widening hands control back to T10.** Once the ladder produces survivors, T10's existing delivery rule runs unchanged: ≤4 speak all, >4 speak top 3 by specificity plus the line saying more matched than could be read. **The widening ladder has no cap of its own.** The only thing it adds to the terminal is the named-dropped-box clause.
- **Widening can never reintroduce a hard-box violation**, by construction — only soft boxes are dropped — and the widened set still passes T10's speaking rule.

### 2 · The wording lock

T18's body conflates two terminals that must not share wording. There are three, not two:

| Terminal | Fires when | Ranked by | Cap | Read-back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Widened match** | the ladder produced survivors | specificity (T10) | T10's rule | six chunks + keypad sections |
| **Nearest** | the ladder exhausted and survivors are still zero | tally (T09), ties by specificity | **2** | **`summary` only** |
| **Empty** | nothing passes the hard-box miss-set gate | — | 0 | — |

- A **widened match is a real match on a stated-smaller set of boxes** and is spoken as one. A **nearest is not a match at all** and must never borrow the widened wording. This distinction is the whole ticket.
- **Nearest is gated on the miss-set**: a scheme whose miss-set holds a hard box is never spoken (T09). Ranking is tally, which is what *near* means; ties break on specificity. Nothing new is invented.
- **Nearest reads the `summary` chunk only, with no keypad section offers.** Offering to read the eligibility section of a scheme just declared a non-match invites the caller to treat it as one. Restraint at the point of highest untruth risk, and it shortens the least valuable part of the call.
- **Two, and it is a restraint number, not a delivery-shape number.** T10's *2–4* is a shape for matches. Reading more nearests dilutes the disclaimer exactly where the caller is most likely to disengage. If only one scheme passes the gate, speak one; if none, the terminal is Empty.

**The lock is an ordering rule, not a phrasing.** In every non-exact terminal, **the qualifying clause is spoken before the scheme names, never after.** A caller who hangs up mid-line must hang up having heard the disclaimer rather than the promise. This is asserted at build time against the `say(sequence)` line order (T15), and it survives translation because it is a property of order, not of words — which matters precisely because no human reads the translations (T08).

English source lines, negative claim leading in all three:

- **Widened** — *"Nothing matched everything you told me."* → *"I set aside what you told me about your income."* → names
- **Nearest** — *"These two did not match what you told me. I am reading them because they are the closest I have."* → names
- **Empty** — *"Nothing matched, and I have nothing close enough to read."*

- **Every dropped box is named, never summarised.** T10 made naming load-bearing; *"some of what you told me"* is the softening that lets a caller believe more was matched than was. At rung 2 the clauses play back to back. Each is a whole sentence per box, not a carrier with a slot, so T15's inflection rule is satisfied.
- **No line in any terminal contains "eligible", "qualify", "entitled", "you will get" or "you can get", in any language.**

**The second half of the lock is a build gate, and it is this ticket's largest contribution.** T08 removed the human verifier, so nothing currently checks a translated string for an eligibility claim before it is spoken to a citizen. Add a **third build gate, the same shape as T06's alias floor and T15's render gate**: a per-language forbidden-phrase check over **read-back chunks and terminal lines only** — not question lines, which must address the caller. It targets second-person eligibility claims (*आप पात्र हैं*, *तुम्ही पात्र आहात*). A scheme whose `summary` trips it does not enter the snapshot. It needs no language skill, runs at build, and is the only mechanical defence of never-mislead while the truth lock is suspended.

**No external phone number, helpline or website in any spoken line for 12 Sep.** Unverifiable referral content is exactly the untruth this ticket exists to close. Recorded as fog: a verified next-step referral is real value and needs a source.

### 3 · Keypad-only mode

**Two ladders exist and were being muddled. They are kept apart:**

- **Box-level drop (T11):** two consecutive non-ANSWER turns on one box move that box to the keypad. The call continues and the model is still used for every other box. Cardinality > 9 sends the box to UNKNOWN instead (T15).
- **Call-level keypad-only (T18):** the model is not called again for the remainder of the call.

Triggers:

- **Model: two failures per call, not consecutive.** Consecutive would let an alternating pattern burn eight turns. A **failure** is any turn producing no usable model output — the 2.0 s hard timeout (T14), a transport error, malformed output, an out-of-set value caught by code, or a span-guard drop. T17 froze *Model never raises and never returns out-of-set*, so these surface as a typed failure return, not an exception, and the counter lives in Engine's loop.
- **An UNCLEAR is not a failure.** It is a successful model turn reporting that it could not hear, and it belongs to T11's box-level ladder. Counting it here would drop healthy calls on a noisy line.
- **ASR: one unrecovered socket.** One immediate reconnect is allowed and consumes no turn; if it fails, drop. The asymmetry with the model is deliberate — a model failure loses a turn's value, an ASR failure loses the channel, and testing a dead socket a second time spends a full turn of dead air to learn something binary.
- **An unhelpful transcript is never an ASR failure.** T14 already assigned those: `vad.speech_start` with an empty or span-less final is NOISE, no `speech_start` at all is SILENCE. Those are caller behaviours bounded by the cap and the silence ladder.
- **Plivo provider error is out of scope here.** A dead Plivo socket is a dead call, not a degraded one — `keepCallAlive="false"` (T14) ends it. It writes no closing line, the LOG shows a call stopping mid-turn, and T04 correctly scores it a fail for ending before a terminal state.

**The caller is told, in one sentence:** *"I am having trouble hearing you. From now on, please answer with the keypad — I will tell you which key each time."* The second clause promises the affordance and earns its length. One fixed line × 3.

**Keypad-only is a mode, not a terminal state — this corrects T16.** T16's handoff line reads that a keypad-only drop *is* a terminal state writing a closing line. It is not: the call continues and reaches an ordinary terminal. What T11 and T16 actually wanted is that a call *ending* in keypad-only scores as a pass, and it does — none of T04's three fail conditions fire. Mechanically: **entering keypad-only writes one line once, on T24's language-switch precedent, and consumes no turn**, since the cap is a predicate on `turn_n`. No new field type, no schema fight.

**One consequence must be spoken rather than silently applied.** In keypad-only, `state` has 36 values and no honest keypad menu, so T15's cardinality rule sends it to UNKNOWN; T11's amendment makes UNKNOWN not-satisfied for T10's speaking rule, so **only schemes that are `ANY` on `state` can be spoken.** That is correct — the alternative speaks a scheme the caller is geographically barred from — but it is an exclusion the caller never hears about. One clause at the terminal, `state` only, since it is the only box that trips the rule: *"I could not get your state, so I only looked at schemes for the whole country."* One line × 3 turns a silent exclusion into a stated one.

### 4 · Keypad-only completes a whole call — confirmed against T09

Traced end to end: questions become keypad menus; `state` goes to UNKNOWN; filter, minimax scoring, stopping, widening and specificity ordering are pure code (T09, T17); read-back is pre-rendered files (T15); *"anything else?"* is a keypad yes/no; the closing line and hangup are unchanged. **Nothing in the path after the opener needs the model.** T18's default holds.

- **The worst case is a drop at turn 1** — no Door A, no box 0 `category`, and T11's *ladder starts from what the opener filled* amendment gets an empty vector instead. Five askable boxes at keypad cost 1 = 5 turns against a cap of 8. **It fits, and it is the binding case.**
- **Door A is unavailable in keypad-only**, by the same cardinality rule that removes `state`: fifty scheme ids have no honest keypad menu. Derived from an existing rule, not invented. **Not announced** — explaining a lost capability the caller may never have wanted costs a line and buys nothing. Recorded consequence: a caller who named a scheme in the opener and hit a model failure will be asked a question instead, and will feel unheard. Accepted for 12 Sep.
- **T11 needs one completion, supplied here**: for a box **already on the keypad**, two non-ANSWER turns drop it to **UNKNOWN**, not to keypad — there is nowhere else to go. An out-of-menu digit (a digit 0–9 not in the menu just offered) is one such non-ANSWER; the question re-plays once, exactly as rung 1 of T14's silence ladder does, using existing inventory.

### 5 · Self-initiated hangup and the last thing heard

Three ways the system ends a call on its own: **silence ladder rung 3** (T14), **the 10-minute ceiling** (T14), and **normal completion** of any of the three terminals plus the *"anything else?"* round.

- **All three end with the same closing line**, in the caller's chosen language, with the hangup waiting on its `playedStream` — T14's rule that nothing hangs up on a queued buffer applies to every terminal without exception.
- **No per-terminal goodbye variants.** A distinct farewell per failure mode tells the caller which failure they hit, which is information for us and is already in the LOG. It would also add three files to T15 to communicate nothing the caller can act on.
- A Plivo socket death produces **no** closing line. That is a T04 fail, and correctly so.

### 6 · T12's handoff — does a Door A caller ever get Door B?

**Yes, and it costs nothing new.** The *"anything else?"* line (rev 2) already runs after every read-back, Door A's included; it clears only the need box, so a Door A caller answering *yes* starts Door B from a near-empty vector at normal price. It falls after `t_end`, so T12's 20 s bar is untouched. **The route existed and only needed someone to say so — the fourth time on this map a dependency ran the wrong way**, after T12→T07, T23→T17 and T22→T07. The fog patch clears with no ticket.
