---

## 1 · What HAQDAAR is, in one paragraph

Someone in India calls a phone number. A voice asks them a few short questions — which state,
how old, what work they do. After about six questions it names the government schemes that match
what they said, and reads out the benefit, who it is for, what papers are needed, and how to
apply. It speaks English, Hindi and Marathi. **It never tells anyone they will get money.** It
only says which schemes match the answers they gave, and it reads the government's own words
back to them.

## 2 · What is built, and what each piece does

Twelve steps of the twenty-step plan are written. Here is every piece and what it is for.

### The rules everyone obeys — `haqdaar/contracts/`

Think of this as the rulebook nobody is allowed to argue with. It holds no logic, only shapes
and numbers.

| File | Lines | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `types.py` | 206 | The shape of everything that moves between the parts — a caller's answers, a scheme, a turn's result. If two parts disagree about a shape, this file wins. |
| `tunables.py` | 51 | **Every number in the system, in one place.** The 8-turn cap. The 6-question wall. The 1.2-second answer budget. The reason they live here is so you can change one mid-demo without touching code. |
| `log_schema.py` | 109 | The shape of a line in the call record. Seven kinds of line. Every turn writes one. |

### The brain — `haqdaar/engine/`

These three files are pure. They do no network, no files, no audio. Give them the same input
twice and they give you the same answer twice. That is why they were easy to build and easy to
prove.

| File | Lines | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `filter.py` | 357 | Holds every scheme as a row of yes/no columns. When the caller says "Bihar", it crosses out every scheme that is not Bihar. It only ever narrows; it never guesses. |
| `planner.py` | 260 | Decides the next question to ask. It picks the question that will cross out the most schemes no matter how the caller answers — worst case, not best case. That is why six questions is enough. It also decides when to stop asking. |
| `terminals.py` | 448 | Writes the ending. There are five endings: an exact match, too many matches, a match after loosening a question, a near match, and nothing found. Each ending has a fixed shape and fixed words. |

### The data — `haqdaar/data/`

| File | Lines | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `pipeline/p1_scrape.py` | 376 | **Built yesterday.** Opens a real browser with no window, visits 12 scheme pages on `myscheme.gov.in`, and saves the five English blocks from each one: benefits, who it is for, who it is not for, papers needed, how to apply. Waits 2 seconds between pages so we are not rude to the site. Never refetches a page read in the last day. If a page fails, it stops and writes nothing — it never makes anything up. |
| `pipeline/schemes.yaml` | 18 | The list of 12 scheme names to fetch. Four were dead links and were swapped; each swap is written down in a comment next to it. |
| `pipeline/p6_snapshot.py` | 544 | Freezes the finished scheme data into a numbered, sealed set. A call is pinned to one snapshot, so the data cannot shift under a live call. |
| `corpus.py` | 346 | Reads a frozen snapshot at startup and hands schemes to the brain. |
| `pool.py` (in `audio/`) | 232 | Keeps the most-used audio clips in memory and loads the rest from disk only when needed, so the first second of a call is not slow. |

### The proof

**64 tests, all passing.** They are not decoration. They are the only reason we can change
something two days before a demo without holding our breath.

## 3 · What the thing can actually do today

**Honestly: it has a brain and it has data, and they have not been introduced yet.**

✅ It can decide. Give the brain a caller's answers and it will narrow 5 schemes down correctly,
pick the smartest next question, and write a correct ending. Proved by 64 tests.

✅ It can read the government site. `make pipeline-scrape` fetches 12 real schemes with real
text. You can open `data_cache/raw/pm-kisan.json` right now and read the actual words.

❌ It cannot hold a conversation. The pieces are not wired into a loop yet. **That is Step 6, and
it is today's first job.**

❌ It cannot speak or listen. No voice yet — that is Steps 9 and 10.

❌ It cannot answer a phone. No Twilio number, no ngrok tunnel. **That is waiting on you, not on
code.**

## 7 · Three things worth knowing before you touch anything

1. **The Groq limit that bites is 1,000 requests per day, not tokens per minute.** One uncached
   pass over the schemes can eat a big slice of it. This is why Step 8 must cache to disk and why
   a second run must cost nothing.

2. **The Groq model returns nothing on its default setting.** Measured live on 11 Sep: the whole
   token budget goes into a hidden thinking field and the answer comes back empty. Send
   `reasoning_effort: "low"` and it returns clean JSON in about half a second.

3. **`data_cache/` is not in git, on purpose.** It is scraped output. It rebuilds from cache in
   about thirty seconds. Never `git add` it.
